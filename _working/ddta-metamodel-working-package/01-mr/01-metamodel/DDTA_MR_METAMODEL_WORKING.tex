\section{DDTA - Working Metamodel - Macro
Requirement}\label{ddta---working-metamodel---macro-requirement}

\textbf{DOCUMENTATION-LAYER CLOSURE CANDIDATE - REVISION 8}

\begin{quote}
This revision consolidates the Macro Requirement semantics after the
Functional Requirement study. It keeps ThreatForge-specific observations
outside the general metamodel and introduces project problem framing as
a method precondition, not as a new governed document type.
\end{quote}

\subsection{1. Scope and layering}\label{1-scope-and-layering}

The conceptual model describes governed project knowledge before fixing
concrete Markdown/YAML encoding or tool behavior. The Doc-as-Code
representation remains versionable, diffable and reviewable; a tool may
validate or assist the model but does not own project semantics.

The layers remain:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{L1 conceptual metamodel} - meaning, concepts, relations,
  cardinalities and invariants;
\item
  \textbf{L2 canonical representation} - concrete fields, headings,
  registries and encoding;
\item
  \textbf{L3 realization principles} - resolution, validation,
  projections and authoring assistance;
\item
  \textbf{L4 tool support/conformance} - which contracts a tool
  supports.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{2. Project problem framing - required method
precondition}\label{2-project-problem-framing---required-method-precondition}

Before decomposing Macro Requirements, the author states the general
problem or class of problems addressed by the project and its meaningful
boundary, temporarily removing current solution choices.

\subsubsection{Fundamental framing
question}\label{fundamental-framing-question}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Which general problem or class of problems must the project
address, within which meaningful boundary, if current solution choices
are temporarily removed?}
\end{quote}

The framing:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  uses problem/domain vocabulary rather than current architecture
  vocabulary;
\item
  prevents chosen devices, providers, algorithms, databases or analysis
  methods from becoming accidental MR semantics;
\item
  is used to test completeness, overlap and stability of the MR set;
\item
  is \textbf{not yet} a \texttt{ProjectProblem} or
  \texttt{ProjectContext} governed-document type.
\end{itemize}

The framing is written once for the project. It is not copied into every
MR.

\subsection{3. Progressive governed-document
hierarchy}\label{3-progressive-governed-document-hierarchy}

For the thesis baseline, the governed authoring hierarchy is
intentionally regular:

\begin{Shaded}
\begin{Highlighting}[]
\NormalTok{MacroRequirement}
\NormalTok{    |}
\NormalTok{    +{-}{-} Decision *}
\NormalTok{           |}
\NormalTok{           +{-}{-} FunctionalRequirement *}
\NormalTok{                  |}
\NormalTok{                  +{-}{-} SpecializedRequirement *}
\end{Highlighting}
\end{Shaded}

The hierarchy is semantic containment, not identifier-string parsing or
folder inference.

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  MR establishes one stable macro responsibility within the framed
  problem.
\item
  Decision records the governed commitment that narrows that
  responsibility.
\item
  FR states the operational behavior required under that commitment.
\item
  Specialized Requirements constrain/enrich an FR. For the thesis
  implementation/evaluation path, the next specialization to formalize
  is \texttt{SecurityRequirement}.
\end{itemize}

No Decision is a child of an FR. If a later choice changes the design,
it remains a Decision under the relevant MR and the affected FR branch
is revised/superseded accordingly.

\subsection{4. Macro Requirement
definition}\label{4-macro-requirement-definition}

A Macro Requirement is a governed root document that owns one durable
macro responsibility or concern needed to address the framed project
problem. It explains the macro purpose/value, minimum context, affected
stakeholders and semantic boundary without selecting lower-level
solution commitments or stating independently testable functional
behavior.

\subsubsection{Fundamental MR question}\label{fundamental-mr-question}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Within the framed project problem, which stable macro
responsibility or concern are we governing, what macro result/value must
it contribute, why does it matter, and who is involved?}
\end{quote}

\subsection{5. Semantic shape}\label{5-semantic-shape}

\begin{longtable}[]{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule\noalign{}
Concept & State & Purpose \\
\midrule\noalign{}
\endhead
\bottomrule\noalign{}
\endlastfoot
\texttt{id} & required & stable identity, history and traceability \\
\texttt{title} & required & concise name understandable within the
project domain \\
\texttt{lifecycle} & required & current/historical governance state \\
\texttt{intent} & required & macro purpose, value and desired outcome \\
\texttt{context} & required & minimum background needed to interpret the
concern \\
\texttt{stakeholders} & required & people/groups that benefit, use,
govern or are affected \\
\texttt{scope} & semantically required & boundary of the macro concern;
exact L2 encoding remains representational \\
\texttt{assumptions} & optional & facts treated as true for the whole
branch \\
\texttt{constraints} & optional & limits applying to the whole branch \\
\texttt{dependsOn} & \texttt{MacroRequirement\ {[}0..*{]}} &
non-hierarchical dependency on another macro responsibility \\
\end{longtable}

\texttt{Objective} remains removed. \texttt{Intent} owns macro purpose,
value and desired outcome.

\subsection{6. MR authoring questions}\label{6-mr-authoring-questions}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  What general problem/class did the project framing establish?
\item
  Which durable responsibility inside that problem does this MR own?
\item
  Can a domain-competent stakeholder understand
  \texttt{title\ +\ Intent} without knowing the current implementation?
\item
  Does the MR remain valid if lower-level providers, devices,
  algorithms, deployment or architecture change?
\item
  Does Context contain only information required to interpret the macro
  concern rather than the current design state?
\item
  Who benefits, governs, uses or is materially affected?
\item
  What is inside/outside this MR\textquotesingle s responsibility
  boundary?
\item
  Is another MR genuinely complementary/dependent? If yes, use
  \texttt{dependsOn}, not false hierarchy or duplicate ownership.
\item
  Has any significant solution choice leaked into MR prose? If so, move
  it to Decision.
\item
  Has any independently assessable behavior leaked into MR prose? If so,
  move it downstream to an FR under a Decision.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{7. Closed MR invariants for the documentation
baseline}\label{7-closed-mr-invariants-for-the-documentation-baseline}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Root hierarchy} - MR has no hierarchical parent.
\item
  \textbf{Stable identity} - identity is independent from
  textual/composite encoding.
\item
  \textbf{Problem anchoring} - every MR is meaningful inside the project
  problem framing.
\item
  \textbf{Single macro concern} - one coherent macro responsibility per
  MR.
\item
  \textbf{Child narrowing} - Decisions and their FR descendants narrow
  the MR rather than introduce unrelated scope.
\item
  \textbf{No solution commitment leakage} - chosen
  architecture/provider/algorithm/mechanism belongs to Decision unless
  intrinsic to the problem domain itself.
\item
  \textbf{No FR-level obligation} - atomic/operational independently
  assessable behavior belongs downstream.
\item
  \textbf{Broad domain readability} - MR can be understood by
  stakeholders competent in the problem domain.
\item
  \textbf{Architecture resilience} - changing lower-level solution
  choices should not normally require rewriting the MR.
\item
  \textbf{Temporal stability} - taking/revising a lower-level Decision
  should not make MR prose obsolete unless the macro concern changes.
\item
  \textbf{Explicit dependencies} - cross-MR complementarity/dependency
  is not hidden as pseudo-hierarchy.
\item
  \textbf{Teachability} - titles + Intent across the MR set support a
  useful macro project map.
\item
  \textbf{Analysis enabling, not analysis producing} - MR provides
  governed context but does not instantiate canonical analysis elements
  or method-specific semantics.
\item
  \textbf{Projection honesty} - a teaching MR map is not a DFD,
  architecture model or threat model.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{8. MR split/merge tests}\label{8-mr-splitmerge-tests}

Before splitting or merging candidate MRs, test:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{independent value} - does each candidate express a
  project-level result meaningful on its own?
\item
  \textbf{Decision-family coherence} - do its descendant commitments
  form a coherent family?
\item
  \textbf{solution-vocabulary removal} - does the distinction survive
  after removing current solution names?
\item
  \textbf{dependency versus containment} - can the relation be expressed
  more accurately as \texttt{dependsOn}?
\item
  \textbf{stakeholder explanation} - can a domain stakeholder explain
  the distinction from title + Intent?
\item
  \textbf{merge stress} - would merging force substantially different
  responsibilities/Decision families into one MR?
\item
  \textbf{split stress} - is separation driven by a project
  responsibility rather than an architectural subsystem?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{9. Analysis boundary}\label{9-analysis-boundary}

MRs may later support Base Analysis construction through governed
downstream documentation, but they do not directly create canonical BAE,
flows, boundaries or method-specific interpretations merely because
nouns appear in MR prose.

The next research phase will formalize Base Analysis/BAE and overlay
behavior without changing this writing model unless a concrete
counterexample requires reopening it.

\subsection{10. Representation deliberately
deferred}\label{10-representation-deliberately-deferred}

L2 still decides:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  where MR concepts live between registry and Markdown;
\item
  exact \texttt{scope} syntax;
\item
  whether parent/null values are materialized;
\item
  concrete \texttt{dependsOn} encoding;
\item
  any identifier format that mirrors hierarchy.
\end{itemize}

Identity/topology must remain semantic relations rather than
consequences of filenames or composite IDs.
