\section{DDTA - Working Metamodel -
Decision}\label{ddta---working-metamodel---decision}

\textbf{DOCUMENTATION-LAYER CLOSURE CANDIDATE - REVISION 8}

Revision 8 preserves the Revision 7 local field core and layering
separation, but refines the definition of Decision after the Functional
Requirement experiments. The thesis baseline intentionally keeps a
regular \texttt{MR\ -\textgreater{}\ Decision\ -\textgreater{}\ FR}
authoring layer.

\subsection{1. Definition}\label{1-definition}

A Decision is a governed \textbf{significant commitment} that narrows
exactly one parent Macro Requirement by fixing a meaningful position,
responsibility boundary, policy, convention, strategy,
technology/architecture choice or other project commitment whose
consequences matter downstream.

Normally the commitment selects among plausible alternatives. In the
rare case where no materially distinct alternative is practical or
admissible, DDTA still permits an explicit \textbf{necessity/default
Decision} so that the documentation layer remains regular and the
commitment is not hidden.

\subsubsection{Fundamental question}\label{fundamental-question}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Which significant commitment narrows this Macro Requirement, why
must the project take that position, and which consequences do we
accept?}
\end{quote}

For an ordinary choice, Context should expose the unresolved
alternatives/tension. For a necessity/default Decision, Context must
explain why alternatives are absent, non-material or excluded by a
governed constraint.

\subsection{2. Semantic core}\label{2-semantic-core}

\begin{Shaded}
\begin{Highlighting}[]
\NormalTok{Decision}
\NormalTok{|{-} title}
\NormalTok{|{-} context}
\NormalTok{|{-} decision}
\NormalTok{\textasciigrave{}{-} consequences}
\end{Highlighting}
\end{Shaded}

All four are semantically required and non-null.

Topology:

\begin{Shaded}
\begin{Highlighting}[]
\NormalTok{MacroRequirement 1 {-}{-}{-}{-} * Decision 1 {-}{-}{-}{-} * FunctionalRequirement}
\end{Highlighting}
\end{Shaded}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  every Decision has exactly one natural MR semantic owner;
\item
  every Functional Requirement has exactly one parent Decision;
\item
  an authored Decision may temporarily have zero FR children;
\item
  Decision is never a child of Functional Requirement.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{3. Closed Decision
semantics}\label{3-closed-decision-semantics}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \texttt{D8-CLOSED-01} - one significant commitment narrowing one
  parent MR;
\item
  \texttt{D8-CLOSED-02} - minimal semantic core is
  \texttt{title/context/decision/consequences};
\item
  \texttt{D8-CLOSED-03} - Context is Decision-local;
\item
  \texttt{D8-CLOSED-04} - one coherent chosen/committed position;
\item
  \texttt{D8-CLOSED-05} - material consequences/trade-offs/downstream
  effects are represented;
\item
  \texttt{D8-CLOSED-06} - no mandatory embedded Alternatives or separate
  Rationale field;
\item
  \texttt{D8-CLOSED-07} - exactly one natural MR semantic owner;
\item
  \texttt{D8-CLOSED-08} - each Decision contributes non-redundant
  governed knowledge;
\item
  \texttt{D8-CLOSED-09} - FRs descend from Decision; a Decision may own
  \texttt{0..*} FRs;
\item
  \texttt{D8-CLOSED-10} - no Decision is hierarchically below an FR.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{4. Genuine-choice Decision versus necessity/default
Decision}\label{4-genuine-choice-decision-versus-necessitydefault-decision}

The normal case remains a genuine project choice.

Use a \textbf{necessity/default Decision} only when a regular Decision
layer is required but the author cannot identify a materially distinct
admissible alternative after reasonable domain review, or when external
constraints effectively reduce the admissible solution set to one
position.

A necessity/default Decision is valid only if:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Context names the reason} - e.g. regulatory constraint,
  contractual boundary, physical impossibility, fixed upstream
  dependency, or explicitly reviewed absence of material alternatives;
\item
  \textbf{Decision states an actual commitment} - not a tautology such
  as "the system will do what the MR says";
\item
  \textbf{Consequences remain material} - the commitment still creates
  downstream responsibilities/FRs or constrains later design;
\item
  \textbf{Reopen trigger is visible} - if the constraining condition
  changes or a material alternative appears, the Decision is
  reviewable/supersedable;
\item
  \textbf{No ignorance laundering} - "we did not think about
  alternatives" is insufficient evidence for this mode.
\end{enumerate}

The metamodel does not require a \texttt{decision\_kind} field at L1.
The distinction can be expressed in Context/Decision/Consequences unless
later representation evidence justifies a dedicated field.

\subsection{5. Decision split/merge
tests}\label{5-decision-splitmerge-tests}

Split commitments that can change independently and have distinct
consequences/trade-offs. Do not merge choices merely because the same FR
is affected by both.

Questions:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  What responsibility/solution space is still unresolved inside the MR?
\item
  What changes if a different position is adopted?
\item
  Does the Decision establish who owns the behavior (internal system vs
  external service/provider)?
\item
  Does it choose a strategy/policy whose operational consequences appear
  in FRs?
\item
  Could part of this Decision change without changing the rest? If yes,
  consider splitting.
\item
  If the Decision is removed, is non-derivable governed commitment
  knowledge lost?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{6. Historical and cross-cutting
boundaries}\label{6-historical-and-cross-cutting-boundaries}

Lifecycle/status, semantic authority, representation, executable
projections and tool support remain cross-cutting L1/L2/L3/L4 concerns
rather than Decision-specific fields.

Large-corpus similarity/search may surface review candidates but does
not decide ownership, equivalence or parentage.
