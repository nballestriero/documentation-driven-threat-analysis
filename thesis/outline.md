# Thesis outline

## 1. Introduction
Problem, motivation, research questions, contributions, scope, and thesis structure.

## 2. Background
Threat modeling, security requirements engineering, traceability, model-driven
security, documentation governance, and continuous security.

## 3. State of the art and research gap
Systematic comparison of starting artifacts, models, analysis methods, outputs,
traceability, automation, evolution, and human review.

## 4. Research method
Research design, case selection, baselines, data collection, coding protocol,
reference models, metrics, validity threats, and reproducibility.

## 5. Documentation-Driven Threat Analysis
Definitions, input contract, Base Analysis metamodel, derivation lifecycle,
overlays, provenance, review states, and change-impact semantics.

## 6. ThreatForge reference implementation
Architecture and implemented mechanisms, described only as evidence that the
method can be operationalized.

## 7. Evaluation
Study results for derivability, consistency, coverage, effort, stale detection,
traceability, and expert review.

## 8. Discussion
Interpretation, comparison with related work, limitations, transferability,
ethical and automation considerations.

## 9. Conclusions
Answers to research questions, contributions, limitations, and future research.
