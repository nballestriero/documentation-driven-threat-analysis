## Why this source is central

SRC-0038 closes the 21-source central-reading corpus with direct evidence about the temporal validity,
maintainability and independent replay of commercial-LLM experiments. Earlier sources show that an
LLM can produce readable candidate requirements or models while remaining semantically incomplete,
incorrect or weakly grounded. SRC-0038 adds a downstream question:

```text
can another team still execute the experiment and obtain compatible evidence?
```

The paper begins with 85 LLM-centric studies from ICSE 2024 and a badge-filtered ASE 2024 sample.
Sixty-nine use OpenAI services. Eighteen initially appear to provide suitable research artifacts, but
only five are sufficiently complete and executable. None is completely reproduced; two are classified
as partially reproducible and three as not reproducible.

The strongest DDTA rules are:

```text
published result + available repository != reproducible evidence
artifact badge at T0 != executable artifact at T1
model identifier != immutable model behavior
```

The paper is not used to require a universal scientific-reproduction platform in the thesis. It is
used to require that DDTA and the parallel ThreatForge project preserve enough execution provenance,
raw evidence, versioning and stale-state information for later replay or independent evaluation.

## Four reading questions

### 1. Research problem and contribution

Commercial LLM studies combine intrinsic non-determinism with opaque provider updates, deprecation,
prompt sensitivity and extensive configuration. Without executable artifacts and sufficiently precise
reporting, a result may become a time-bound observation rather than cumulative scientific evidence.

The paper asks:

1. to what extent LLM-centric empirical software-engineering studies are reproducible;
2. which factors impede reproduction;
3. whether ACM artifact badges reliably indicate reproducibility.

The contribution combines a critical review of 85 articles, attempted reproduction of 18 apparently
eligible studies, detailed reproduction of five executable studies, a container-based framework and
recommendations for authors, venues and funding agencies.

### 2. Starting artifacts and study-selection boundary

The ICSE branch searches 425 articles, retrieves 109 keyword matches and manually retains 78 empirical
LLM studies. The ASE branch begins with 314 articles, finds 108 LLM studies and intentionally retains
only seven with ACM artifact badges. The combined corpus is therefore useful for critical reflection,
but it is not a uniform systematic sample of both venues.

The main funnel is:

```text
85 analyzed studies
-> 69 use OpenAI services
-> 18 appear to provide complete and executable artifacts
-> 5 are actually executable enough for reproduction
```

The OpenAI-only reproduction boundary improves setup comparability but limits generalization to other
providers and to open-weight models.

### 3. Reproduction procedure, provenance and human intervention

Five researchers build a containerized framework. Original artifacts are downloaded on demand rather
than silently repackaged. Where necessary, integrations recover dependencies, reconstruct missing
parts or automate manual steps. Every integration and intervention is reviewed by another coauthor.

The procedure is:

```text
retrieve original artifact
-> verify code and data completeness
-> execute unchanged where possible
-> document blockers
-> make functionality-preserving interventions
-> containerize and automate
-> independent code review
-> repeat runs
-> preserve observations and generated evidence
```

The distinction between the original artifact and the repaired reproduction environment is essential.
A replacement model, reconstructed evaluator or dependency repair changes the evidential conditions
and must remain explicit.

Each executable study is run up to 30 times or until its reproduction cost reaches 500 USD. The cap is
pragmatic rather than statistically sufficient. The authors do not contact original study authors,
thereby testing package self-sufficiency but potentially classifying as blocked an experiment that
could be recovered with author assistance.

For comparison, the authors apply descriptive analysis and a Bayesian bootstrap with 10,000 draws.
Original metrics inside the reproduced 95 percent interval are treated as likely; values outside it are
treated as unlikely. The method uses observed runs only and cannot guarantee coverage of rare or
extreme LLM behavior.

### 4. Results, limitations and relationship with DDTA

#### Overall result

| Stage | Count |
|---|---:|
| LLM-centric studies analyzed | 85 |
| Studies using OpenAI services | 69 |

| Sufficiently complete and executable | 5 |

#### Five executable studies

- Pan et al., code translation: 15 repetitions, about 450 USD, not reproduced. Reported successful-translation ratios of 79.9 and 85.5 percent are far outside reproduced intervals near 46-55 percent.
- Ahmed et al., code summarization: 30 repetitions, about 30 USD, not reproduced. Deprecated and unspecified model versions, incomplete instructions and dependency reconstruction materially affect the setup.
- Siddiq et al., regex generation and ReDoS evaluation: 30 repetitions, about 30 USD. Five reported values appear likely, while 21 are outside reproduced intervals; evaluation code and one metric cannot be fully reconstructed.
- Zhou et al., vulnerability detection: 30 repetitions across eight configurations, about 36 USD, partially reproduced. Some metrics vary by 10-30 percentage points across runs.
- Xiao et al., log parsing: 30 repetitions, about 40 USD, partially reproduced. Most evaluated combinations are close to or inside reproduced intervals, but one metric and part of the original setup remain unavailable.

#### Reporting and artifact state across 85 studies

| Information or condition | Count |
|---|---:|
| No shared code or data | 35 |
| Irrecoverably incomplete artifact | 15 |
| Model not identified | 8 |
| Temperature reported | 29 |
| Top-p or top-k reported | 14 |
| Context-window handling described | 22 |
| Prompt or prompt template reported | 50 |

Prompt disclosure alone is insufficient. Reproduction also depends on exact model identity, context
handling, inputs, preprocessing, evaluator code, dependency versions, runtime, retries and repeated
raw outputs.

#### Main blockers among the 18 apparently eligible studies

- incomplete artifacts: six studies;
- dependency-version problems: six;
- general code problems: five;
- deprecated models: five;
- incomplete documentation: four;
- non-executable artifacts: two;
- insufficient error handling: two;
- deprecated external API: one.

Only model deprecation is specific to commercial LLMs. The remaining blockers are conventional
software-engineering and artifact-maintenance failures.

#### Artifact badges

Sixteen studies have an ACM `Artifacts Evaluated - Reusable` badge. Four later expose incomplete
artifacts, three are non-functional and one lacks sufficient reproduction documentation. Half of the
badged artifacts therefore fail to satisfy the expected reusable state about one year later. The paper
cannot determine whether this is caused by weak initial evaluation, external decay, repository expiry,
dependency changes or model deprecation.

## Internal discrepancies and ambiguities

1. The reviewed PDF consistently uses 85 studies and reconstructs the total as 78 ICSE plus 7 ASE.
   External conference metadata encountered during candidate verification reported 86. The PDF value
   is internally supported, while the external discrepancy is preserved.
2. The text says Ahmed et al. do not specify a precise `gpt-3.5-turbo` version, while Table 3 appears
   to mark `Minor Version Set` as `Yes` for that study.
3. In the Xiao et al. discussion, the text says two close combinations but names three, then says five
   unlikely combinations but the visible list names four.
4. Reproducibility and replicability are intentionally used interchangeably in a grey area involving
   substituted models and repaired artifacts. Exact ACM terminology must therefore not be inferred
   from every later use of the words.
5. The 30-run and 500-USD thresholds are practical budget decisions, not universal statistical rules.
6. ASE selection is pre-filtered by artifact badge and is not complete in the same way as the ICSE
   search branch.
7. A result inside a reproduced 95 percent interval can still have operationally large variability;
   interval inclusion is not equivalent to stable behavior.
8. Badge failure is observational. The study does not establish which actor or lifecycle event caused
   the artifact to cease meeting reusable expectations.

## Limitations stated or supported by the source

- only OpenAI-based studies are taken into the reproduction stage;
- only ICSE 2024 and a selected ASE 2024 subset are considered;
- only five studies are executable enough for the detailed result comparison;
- experiments and datasets are often reproduced only in part;
- runs are capped at 30 and by budget;
- model substitutions and code repairs weaken exact equivalence to the original setup;
- original authors are not contacted;
- Bayesian bootstrap intervals use only observed outputs;
- metric variation reaches about 30 percentage points in some configurations;
- artifact decay, provider drift and reproduction-team intervention cannot always be separated;
- the reproduction framework and companion data were not independently executed during DDTA reading.

## DDTA interpretation

### Execution evidence is a versioned artifact

An LLM result must be represented as a versioned execution event, not as a permanent property of a
model family. Minimum provenance includes:

- provider and exact observed model identifier or snapshot;
- API and endpoint identity;
- system prompt, user prompt and prompt-template version;
- sampling and tool parameters;
- input snapshot, source identifiers and hashes;
- retrieval index, context-window and truncation policy;
- preprocessing and postprocessing versions;
- dependency lock, runtime or container identity and SBOM;
- evaluator implementation and metric definition;
- run count, timestamps, costs, errors and retries;
- raw outputs and aggregate result;
- human interventions and corrections;
- original result versus rerun result;
- disposition and stale reason.

### Candidate replay lifecycle

```text
governed source snapshot
-> run specification
-> executable environment
-> repeated executions
-> raw outputs
-> metric computation
-> reported result
-> optional independent rerun
-> not_attempted | reproduced | partial | divergent
   | blocked | unavailable | superseded
-> stale after source, model, prompt, dependency,
   evaluator or environment change
```

This lifecycle does not guarantee deterministic replay. It ensures that uncertainty, change and
failure to replay remain explicit rather than being collapsed into a timeless claim.

### Relationship with earlier central readings

- SRC-0035 shows that a readable behavioral model can remain semantically wrong.
- SRC-0036 shows that parser-valid structural models can encode wrong relations and multiplicities.
- SRC-0037 shows that correct-looking regulatory requirements can have low obligation coverage and
  weak grounding.
- SRC-0038 shows that even a published empirical result and artifact package may not remain executable
  or reproduce compatible evidence.

Together they define four separate acceptance boundaries:

```text
artifact readability
artifact semantic fidelity
source coverage and grounding
execution reproducibility over time
```

## ThreatForge implication and thesis scope

SRC-0038 supports an execution-provenance contract for LLM-assisted extraction, model generation and
future analysis runs. ThreatForge should be able to retain an immutable target-project snapshot,
model and prompt identity, raw candidate output, validation evidence, reviewer disposition and stale
reason. The model should permit a later replay attempt and distinguish its result from the original.

In scope for the thesis:

- complete execution provenance for implemented LLM-assisted operations;
- hashes and versions for governed inputs and produced candidates;
- raw outputs and validation evidence kept distinct;
- explicit human interventions and review disposition;
- stale status and trigger;
- a future-compatible replay status vocabulary.

Out of scope for the thesis:

- a universal framework for reproducing external scientific studies;
- automatic Docker or Zenodo integration;
- preservation of commercial model weights;
- a guarantee of deterministic outputs;
- a universal formula for the correct number of runs;
- automated verification of ACM artifact badges;
- mandatory repeated execution of every ThreatForge analysis during the thesis evaluation.

## Claims this source supports

- artifact availability is weaker than completeness, executability and reproducibility;
- exact model, prompt and context provenance is necessary but not sufficient;
- ordinary software dependencies and code quality are part of LLM reproducibility;
- repeated raw runs are necessary to expose output variability;
- original and reproduced results need separate identities and states;
- model, dependency and API deprecation are stale-state triggers;
- artifact reviews and badges are temporally bounded evidence;
- transparent repair and independent review improve auditability;
- LLM evidence should carry blocked, partial, divergent and unavailable states.

## Claims this source does not support

- all LLM studies are irreproducible;
- all providers behave like OpenAI;
- 30 repetitions are always sufficient;
- containerization guarantees reproducibility;
- an open-weight model is automatically reproducible;
- badge assignment causes later artifact failure;
- a pinned commercial model is empirically immutable;
- the thesis must implement a complete reproduction laboratory.

## Reading disposition

SRC-0038 is accepted as the final central reading. It closes the corpus with direct empirical evidence
for execution provenance, temporal validity, artifact maintenance and independent rerun states. Its
quantitative results are bounded to the sampled 2024 studies and the reproduction conditions used by
the authors. The general design lesson is retained; provider rankings and historical model behavior
are not treated as current 2026 performance evidence.
