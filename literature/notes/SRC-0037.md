## Why this source is central

SRC-0037 provides the direct privacy and non-functional-requirement generation evidence requested after
SRC-0036. It studies a concrete documentation transformation from primary and complementary legal
sources into candidate privacy requirements. Unlike the UML-generation studies, the output is a
normative software obligation rather than a behavioral or structural diagram.

The study is especially useful because it separates several dimensions that can otherwise be
collapsed into a single misleading quality score:

- correctness of the generated requirement;
- coverage of the human reference set;
- validity and completeness of the cited legal reference;
- plausibility of the generated rationale;
- duplicate generation.

The strongest DDTA rule is:

```text
high correctness among generated outputs
!= high coverage of applicable obligations
```

A second rule is:

```text
retrieved context or an explicit citation
!= valid and complete legal grounding
```

The thesis-scope decision is deliberately limited. SRC-0037 does not authorize implementation of a
regulatory-analysis subsystem in DDTA or in the parallel ThreatForge project. It is used to require
future compatibility only: current document, provenance, candidate-review and Base Analysis
boundaries must not prevent a later governed legal-requirement analysis, but no legal source model,
RAG pipeline, compliance scorer, legal plugin or GDPR claim is implemented in the thesis scope.

## Four reading questions

### 1. Research problem and contribution

The paper addresses the knowledge gap between legal interpretation and software development. The
GDPR states data-subject rights at a general level, while implementable software requirements often
need detail from guidelines, judicial decisions, legal literature and expert interpretation.

The authors make three main contributions:

1. a reference set of 108 privacy requirements for the rights of access and portability;
2. explicit links from those requirements to primary and complementary legal sources;
3. XTRAREG, an LLM- and RAG-assisted approach evaluated against the expert-built reference set.

The 108 requirements comprise 61 access requirements and 47 portability requirements. The study is a
case study on two GDPR rights, not a general extraction benchmark for all regulations or all NFRs.

### 2. Starting artifacts and system representation

The manual process uses multiple source classes:

- legislation;
- judicial decisions;
- official guidelines or technical specifications;
- legal literature;
- legal-expert interpretation.

Six subject-matter experts contribute to source selection, extraction, consolidation and refinement.
The requirements preserve source references. The paper distinguishes minimal-compliance obligations
from recommended or best-practice requirements based mainly on the authority of the source.

Three observations are important for documentation-first analysis:

- obligations and best practices must not be conflated;
- general legal text may remain vague until application context is known;
- alternative legal interpretations may both be defensible and should retain their provenance.

The automated transformation is:

```text
manually selected legal sources
-> source indexing and retrieval
-> zero-shot or chain-of-thought prompt
-> GPT-3.5 or GPT-4o
-> generated requirement, legal reference and rationale
-> automated and expert evaluation
```

The preparatory selection of applicable legal sources remains manual. XTRAREG does not decide
jurisdiction, applicability, authority or project context.

### 3. Traceability, change, automation and human role

#### Human reference construction

The access requirements are extracted by one legal expert; portability requirements are independently
extracted by two experts and consolidated. The sets are discussed and iteratively refined in four
face-to-face sessions with additional expert participation. The paper reports no inter-rater
agreement statistic for this phase because disagreements were resolved interactively.

#### Prompting and RAG configuration

The evaluated models are `gpt-3.5-turbo-0125` and `gpt-4o-2024-08-06`. The temperature is 0.3.
Documents are split into 1000-character chunks with 200-character overlap. Retrieval uses the top
four contexts, OpenAI `text-embedding-ada-002` embeddings and Chroma through LangChain. Sources are
processed separately and generated lists are concatenated.

This configuration is execution provenance, not a timeless algorithm. Model, API, library, source,
chunking, retrieval and prompt versions can all alter the result.

#### Evaluation

Automated comparison uses BLEU, ROUGE-L, METEOR and BERTScore. The authors explicitly reject using
only an LLM evaluator because legal equivalence requires expert judgment and LLM judges may favor
their own outputs.

The extrinsic evaluation assesses:

- correctness: correct, partially correct or incorrect;
- groundedness: grounded, incomplete, misgrounded or ungrounded;
- plausibility of the rationale;
- mapping to reference requirements for coverage.

The configurations with RAG are run twice. The ablation study without RAG is run ten times because
it is cheaper. This reduces perfect symmetry between the two experiments.

#### Change and maintenance

The paper recognizes opaque model updates as a validity threat and uses two model generations and
multiple time frames. It does not define legal-source versioning, supersession, effective dates,
change-impact analysis, stale requirement detection or maintained regeneration after regulatory or
project changes.

### 4. Findings, limitations and relationship with DDTA

#### GPT-4o plus ZSL plus RAG

| Dimension | ACC | PRT |
|---|---:|---:|
| Generated requirements | 22 | 14 |
| Duplicates | 8 (36.4%) | 4 (28.6%) |
| Reference requirements identified | 16 | 8 |
| Correct plus partially correct | 18/22 (81.8%) | 12/14 (85.7%) |
| Fully correct only, researcher calculation | 14/22 (63.6%) | 5/14 (35.7%) |
| Grounded references | 15/22 (68.2%) | 7/14 (50.0%) |
| Incomplete references | 6/22 (27.3%) | 3/14 (21.4%) |
| Misgrounded references | 1/22 (4.5%) | 4/14 (28.6%) |

The headline accuracy combines correct and partially correct requirements. The fully-correct-only
rates are researcher calculations from Table III and are not named metrics in the paper.

#### Coverage interpretation

The text reports that 16 of 22 generated ACC requirements and 8 of 14 generated PRT requirements
map to the reference set. Relative to the complete human reference set, however, the identified
coverage is:

```text
ACC: 16 / 61 = 26.2 percent
PRT:  8 / 47 = 17.0 percent
```

A high fraction of acceptable outputs therefore coexists with low obligation coverage.

#### Effect of RAG

RAG improves most automated metrics and increases output quantity. It still fails to guide the model
toward a comprehensive set of requirements beyond the most directly relevant GDPR articles. It
also introduces substantial duplication because outputs from separately processed sources are
concatenated.

RAG does not establish:

- source applicability;
- complete legal grounding;
- correct legal interpretation;
- absence of duplicate requirements;
- coverage of the applicable obligation set;
- implementation-level compliance.

#### Error patterns

The qualitative analysis reports:

- wrong requirement perspective, such as describing exercise of a right rather than system behavior;
- requirements that remain as generic as the legal text;
- omitted exceptions;
- incorrect actions;
- non-actionable verbatim or near-verbatim legal descriptions;
- incomplete or misgrounded references;
- reliance on recitals without sufficient normative provisions.

The paper reports that all rationales were considered plausible. Plausibility is assessed coarsely and
does not imply that the requirement or reference is correct.

## Internal discrepancies and ambiguities

1. The reviewed PDF consistently supports 85.7 percent for PRT, including Table III. Some external
   metadata report 56.7 percent. The discrepancy is preserved and not silently reconciled.
2. The paper labels correct plus partially correct outputs as accuracy; this is broader than a
   fully-correct-only rate.
3. Coverage is discussed both as mapped references relative to generated outputs and as coverage of
   the human reference set. These denominators must be stated explicitly.
4. The evaluation-method section describes one legal expert for the extrinsic assessment, while the
   threats-to-validity section says two experts performed it collectively.
5. The answer to RQ2 says RAG has a significant impact without reporting a statistical significance
   test.
6. The source taxonomy uses `T` for legal literature, while the compliance-scoring discussion later
   lists `D` among advisory and academic sources.
7. Page 10 contains wording that appears to confuse ZSL, ZSL-dagger and ACC in several comparisons.
8. The conclusion rounds ACC accuracy to 81 percent while the rest of the paper reports 81.8 percent.

## Limitations stated or supported by the source

- only the GDPR rights of access and portability are evaluated;
- no concrete software project or implemented system is assessed;
- no compliance determination is performed;
- the reference set is expert-constructed and not necessarily unique;
- legal interpretation and mapping remain subjective;
- generated and reference requirements are not necessarily one-to-one;
- coverage is low relative to the human corpus;
- duplicate generation is substantial;
- model updates and behavior are opaque;
- no correction-effort or accepted-requirement-yield metric is reported;
- no requirements-engineer user study is reported;
- no regulatory-change or stale-state workflow is evaluated;
- the public annex and software artifact were not independently rerun in this reading;
- the model and API versions must not be presented as current 2026 performance evidence.

## Core definitions

### Regulatory requirement candidate

A proposed software obligation generated or extracted from one or more selected legal sources. It is
not an accepted requirement until applicability, authority, wording, actionability, coverage,
grounding and alternative interpretations have been reviewed.

### Legal grounding

Evidence that a cited legal source is valid, relevant and sufficiently complete to support the
requirement as formulated. A retrieved segment or citation string is not itself grounding evidence.

### Obligation coverage

The fraction of the applicable expert-accepted obligation set represented by generated candidates.
It must not be confused with the fraction of generated outputs that happen to match at least one
reference requirement.

### Regulatory staleness

A state indicating that a previously reviewed requirement may need re-evaluation because of changes
to legislation, case law, authoritative guidance, application context, source selection, retrieval
index, prompt, model or acceptance rules.

## Nine documentation-first fields

1. Input and quality: GDPR plus complementary legal sources selected by experts; applicability remains contextual.
2. Compatibility: direct legal-document input; no code or existing system model is required.
3. Intermediate representation: PDF chunks and embeddings; no governed neutral legal IR.
4. Procedure: select sources, index, retrieve, prompt, generate requirements/references/rationales, evaluate and review.
5. Output: privacy requirement candidates with proposed references and rationales.
6. Provenance, uncertainty and review: model and retrieval settings are reported; legal applicability and acceptance remain expert decisions.
7. Evolution/change: model variability is recognized; legal-source and requirement maintenance are not implemented.
8. Evaluation/evidence: 108 expert requirements, two rights, automated metrics, expert correctness, groundedness and coverage.
9. Thesis use: evidence for future-compatible candidate, provenance and review boundaries, not authorization to implement legal analysis.

## Directly supported propositions

- Legal requirements may require authoritative sources beyond the primary regulation.
- Expert collaboration can produce trace-linked privacy requirements from multiple legal source types.
- LLM and RAG assistance can generate correct or partially correct privacy requirements in the evaluated case.
- Generated requirements may be duplicated, incomplete, incorrectly framed or based on weak references.
- Correctness, coverage and groundedness are distinct dimensions.
- RAG improves alignment in the evaluated configurations but does not provide a comprehensive list beyond the GDPR.
- Human legal review remains necessary.
- Model and infrastructure changes threaten reproducibility.

## Not supported

- Autonomous or exhaustive legal-requirement extraction.
- Automatic GDPR compliance.
- Reliability of generated citations by default.
- Immediate implementability of every generated requirement.
- Replacement of legal experts.
- Generalization to all GDPR rights, regulations, jurisdictions or NFR categories.
- Current 2026 superiority of GPT-4o or any other model.
- A requirement that DDTA or ThreatForge implement a legal-analysis subsystem within the thesis.

## Relationship with Documentation-Driven Threat Analysis

### Candidate lifecycle

```text
governed legal source set and applicability decision
-> source versions, authority and effective dates recorded
-> retrieval and generation provenance frozen
-> candidate requirement, reference and rationale generated
-> duplicate and alternative analysis
-> actionability and perspective validation
-> correctness validation
-> obligation-coverage validation
-> legal-grounding validation
-> expert review
-> accepted | corrected | rejected | deferred
-> stale after legal, contextual, source, model, prompt, index or rule change
-> re-review before continued reliance
```

### Required future extension boundary

A future regulatory-analysis extension would need to declare:

- accepted source and jurisdiction types;
- source authority, applicability, version and effective dates;
- selected and excluded sources;
- exact source segments and retrieval evidence;
- provider, model, prompt, embedding, chunking, index and retrieval settings;
- candidate wording, system perspective, rationale and proposed references;
- duplicate, alternative and conflict relationships;
- correctness, actionability, coverage and grounding results;
- expert identity or role, corrections and disposition;
- stale triggers and supersession relationships.

This list is a future-compatibility checklist, not an implementation plan for the thesis.

## Relationship with ThreatForge and thesis scope

The current ThreatForge architecture already separates documentary extraction candidates from
canonical Base Analysis and prevents automatic LLM acceptance. It also keeps Target Project facts
local and keeps methodology-specific analysis separate from the neutral Base Analysis. SRC-0037
supports those boundaries.

The paper does not produce threat findings. It produces legal and privacy requirement candidates.
Those outputs should not be forced into the current methodology-plugin to Common-Finding to
Security-Requirement chain.

For the thesis, the accepted scope is:

```text
in scope
- preserve generic provenance and candidate-review extensibility
- keep Base Analysis neutral and capable of representing future legally motivated actors, data,
  components, boundaries and flows
- avoid coupling threat-analysis plugins to legal extraction
- avoid schemas that make future regulatory source references impossible

out of scope
- regulatory source registry implementation
- legal applicability engine
- GDPR or other legal RAG pipeline
- legal review workflow
- compliance scoring or certification
- new privacy/compliance document model
- regulatory extraction plugin
```

The source is therefore used as a boundary and extensibility argument, not as an additional product
feature commitment.

## Temporal caveat

The paper was published in 2025 but uses fixed OpenAI model snapshots and library versions. Its
separation of correctness, coverage, grounding and review remains useful. Its exact performance,
model comparison and provider behavior must not be treated as current 2026 evidence without a new
replication.
