## Four reading questions

### 1. Research problem and contribution

The paper examines tension between Problem Frames' intention to delay solution thinking and the practical fact that existing architecture can influence feasible development. It proposes an extension that represents architectural services in/around the machine domain so existing support can be considered without simply re-deriving it.

### 2. Starting artifacts and system representation

The paper begins with problem frames composed of machine, real-world domains, interfaces, phenomena, requirements and frame concerns. A warehouse-ordering case is decomposed into commanded-behaviour subproblems. Existing OAAT and FCFS services then create a pressure point: treating them as ordinary given domains becomes awkward, so the authors annotate the machine with architectural services.

### 3. Traceability, change, automation and human role

Analysis and correctness argument remain human/manual. The extension lets the analyst rely on explicit service properties when discharging a frame concern. No automated extraction, provenance or change/governance mechanism is reported.

### 4. Findings, limitations and relationship with DDTA / overlay

The case demonstrates conceptual feasibility on a small example, not general effectiveness. The authors explicitly leave larger cases and methodological consequences for future work. For DDTA the source is useful because it shows that problem and solution concerns can interact without erasing their distinction—and that importing an architectural fact changes the analysis boundary and must be explicit.

## Research method and corpus

Conceptual extension plus small warehouse-ordering case study.

## Contribution

Controlled bridge between problem-frame analysis and pre-existing architectural support.

## Findings

- Problem analysis can be distorted if existing architecture is either ignored or silently embedded.
- Architectural services can be made explicit as assumptions/support in the analysis rather than confused with the original problem semantics.
- Frame concerns/correctness arguments reveal what architectural properties are being relied upon.

## Limitations stated by the authors / accessible source

- Small case study; no comparative/empirical evaluation.
- Extension's adequacy for larger systems left open.
