## Four reading questions

### 1. Research problem and contribution

The accessible material establishes that the book is a broad security-pattern catalogue and that it explicitly separates chapters on identity management, authentication and access control. The Chapter 5 preview states that users/systems first identify themselves and are authenticated; identification/authentication provides the basis for later authorization and logging.

### 2. Starting artifacts and system representation

Only preview material was reviewed. Chapter 4 addresses identity-management infrastructures; Chapter 5 treats Authenticator/Credential patterns; Chapter 6 treats Authorization and access-control models. The previewed authentication flow assumes that a subject identifies itself to the system and then proves that claimed identity.

### 3. Traceability, change, automation and human role

The accessible previews describe pattern relationships and a lifecycle methodology, but the full pattern anatomy and complete procedures were not reviewed. No DDTA-specific traceability or authority mechanism can be attributed to the book from the reviewed preview.

### 4. Findings, limitations and relationship with DDTA / overlay

For R23, the targeted preview is already useful as a terminology pressure test: identification/self-identification, authentication and authorization are not presented as synonyms. However, because chapters 2, 4, 5 and 6 were not fully available, this card must not be treated as a deep reading of the book.
