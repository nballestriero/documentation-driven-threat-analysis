## Four reading questions

### 1. Research problem and contribution

The thesis addresses the lack of a systematic traceable path from requirements to machine specifications. It develops 'problem progression': transform the requirement and problem context step by step until a specification is reached. Two approaches are developed—formal CSP-based progression and a semi-formal causal-rule approach.

### 2. Starting artifacts and system representation

The thesis starts from a Problem Frames problem description: requirement, domains, interfaces/phenomena and domain descriptions. It explicitly adopts Jackson's distinction between a requirement as desired environment behaviour and a specification as machine-interface behaviour. It also reviews problem-frame matching/decomposition and then develops progression as a distinct transformation operation.

### 3. Traceability, change, automation and human role

Progression is represented as a sequence of transformed problem diagrams/requirements. The semi-formal approach uses explicit rule applicability conditions and carries assumptions about removed domains into rewritten requirements to preserve the solution relation. The thesis does not report an automated end-to-end tool; analysts select/apply rules and construct/validate domain descriptions. It also acknowledges that stakeholders may disagree on causal descriptions.

### 4. Findings, limitations and relationship with DDTA / overlay

The thesis provides multiple worked cases and a formal/semi-formal comparison, but it identifies scalability and practicality limits. The formal route can force detailed low-level design and becomes complex; the semi-formal route is broader but depends on causal domain knowledge and still leaves solution synthesis/design choices. The thesis explicitly notes that Jackson's original problem progression was only sketched. For DDTA, its strongest lesson is that moving from governed requirement semantics toward a machine specification requires explicit transformations and assumptions—not silent renaming.

## Research method and corpus

PhD thesis: literature survey, formal CSP semantics/operators, semi-formal causal progression rules and multiple worked case studies.

## Contribution

Systematic requirement-to-specification progression with explicit context transformation and assumptions.

## Findings

- Requirement and machine specification are distinct descriptions connected through the problem context.
- Transforming toward a specification may require rewriting the requirement while recording assumptions that compensate for removed context.
- Pattern/frame matching and problem progression are different activities.
- Formal progression can overcommit to detailed design; semi-formal progression trades rigor for broader applicability.

## Limitations stated by the authors / accessible source

- Case-study validation rather than broad industrial evaluation.
- Causal descriptions can be contested by stakeholders.
- Tool support and scalable automation remained future work.

## Relationship with Documentation-Driven Threat Analysis

### Directly supported propositions

- A requirement-to-specification move is not a wording cleanup; it changes the descriptive locus toward the machine.
- Assumptions used to bridge removed context should be explicit.
- Matching a problem class does not itself produce the final machine specification.

### Tensions or contradictions

- DDTA BA derivation must not accidentally perform hidden problem progression or introduce solution assumptions under the guise of semantic normalization.

### Research gap left open

Governed provenance for every progression/assumption and regression when documentation changes.

### Overlay-method implications

Use as a warning that downstream derivation steps need explicit evidence and should remain distinguishable from source semantics.

## Candidate thesis claims

- Semantic normalization and solution/specification derivation should be separate, reviewable operations.
- Assumption-bearing transformations need traceability.
- A recognised pattern/frame is not equivalent to an automatically justified specification.
