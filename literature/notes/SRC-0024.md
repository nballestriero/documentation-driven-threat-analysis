## Companion repository audit

Researcher observation recorded on 2026-08-04: the companion repository contains method
directories, datasets and result spreadsheets, but its README states that training corpora,
CoreNLP libraries and some large-scale datasets must be supplied separately. It also directs
users to external repositories for the TAROT and FTLR implementations. The package is useful
but is not a self-contained reproduction environment. This observation must remain separate
from the authors' claims in the paper.

## Four reading questions

### 1. Research problem and contribution

The paper addresses two connected problems in requirement-to-code traceability link recovery
(RC-TLR): the absence of a uniform benchmark and the tendency to compare new methods mainly
against old, weak baselines. The authors propose a three-stage framework for collecting the
recent literature, selecting reproducible candidate methods and evaluating them under common
datasets and metrics.

The study searches work published from 2018 to 2023, reduces 3609 initial records to 29
relevant studies, assesses 11 candidate papers and selects six candidate SOTA papers. It then
replicates four information-retrieval method families and evaluates a combined machine-learning
feature set with several classifiers on 13 Java datasets.

### 2. Starting artifacts and system representation

The source artifacts are natural-language requirements and Java source-code artifacts paired
with a reference set of requirement-to-code links. The study uses small COEST datasets and
larger datasets derived from open-source or industrial-style systems. Each potential pair is
treated either as a ranked retrieval candidate or as a binary classification instance.

The paper does not derive an architectural, data-flow or security model. It evaluates whether
an algorithm can recover links between already available requirements and code. Consequently,
its findings apply to trace-candidate recovery and evaluation, not to document-to-Base-Analysis
transformation.

### 3. Traceability, change, automation and human role

The evaluated methods use textual similarity, part-of-speech information, consensual biterms,
fine-grained requirement-code relations, code-artifact closeness and machine-learning features.
The experiments measure precision, recall and F1, examine cutoff versus top-N selection, vary
training/test proportions and test SMOTE data balancing.

The best method changes by dataset. Cutoff selection is sensitive to the dataset and method;
the paper recommends testing every cutoff in a derived interval rather than reporting only one
optimally tuned point. Class imbalance materially affects classifier results. The study does not
measure analyst review time or user trust directly, although precision and recall represent the
false-positive and omission burden that a reviewer would face.

### 4. Findings, limitations and relationship with DDTA / overlay

No universal winner emerges. On the COEST datasets, different combinations of CRT, TAROT,
FTLR and classical IR models lead on different projects. Random forest combined with SMOTE
performs best on most machine-learning experiments. On the larger datasets, the best F1 values
are generally modest and remain strongly dataset-dependent.

For DDTA, the source is mainly an evaluation and baseline-selection reference. It supports
multi-project benchmarking, explicit dataset and ground-truth contracts, versioned baseline
implementations, class-imbalance controls and reporting across thresholds. It does not show
that an automatically recovered link is trustworthy evidence, that an LLM is superior, or that
trace recovery improves threat-analysis quality.

## Research method and corpus

- Six literature databases plus forward and backward snowballing.
- Search window: 2018 to 2023.
- Initial retrieval: 3609 records.
- After screening and snowballing: 29 studies.
- Purpose-based candidate set: 11 papers.
- Final candidate SOTA set: six papers.
- Eligible public datasets: 16; experimental subset: 13 Java datasets.
- Classical baselines: VSM, LSI, LDA and Jensen-Shannon similarity.
- IR method families: Conpos, TAROT, FTLR and CRT.
- ML classifiers: random forest, KNN, logistic regression, naive Bayes, SVM and a hard-voting ensemble.
- Settings include multiple train/test ratios and optional SMOTE balancing.
- Main metrics: precision, recall and F1.
- Mann-Whitney U tests are used for selected ML comparisons.

## Contribution

- A repeatable framework for identifying candidate SOTA methods.
- A consolidated inventory of methods, datasets, metrics and baseline choices.
- Replicated comparisons on small and larger datasets.
- Dataset-specific guidance for baseline selection.
- A public companion repository containing code, datasets and result spreadsheets, with
  reproducibility caveats documented separately above.

## Findings

- No single IR method leads across every dataset.
- On COEST, the best IR F1 values range from 0.3406 to 0.5763 depending on the project.
- Random forest with SMOTE achieves the best ML result on most tested datasets.
- On the larger datasets, best IR F1 values range from 0.0861 to 0.3814.
- On the larger datasets, random forest with SMOTE is usually best, but absolute F1 values
  remain between 0.1551 and 0.3835; Maven is led by the voting ensemble at 0.3099.
- Data balancing helps several boundary-based classifiers but can reduce naive-Bayes performance.
- More training data often helps, but can also increase overfitting.
- Cutoff-based retrieval generally outperforms fixed top-N selection in the tested COEST setting.
- Performance, threshold and baseline choice are project-dependent.

## Limitations stated by the authors

- Literature-quality and evidence scoring retain a subjective component.
- Part-of-speech tagging can be distorted by preprocessing order.
- The Italian eAnci dataset introduces language-processing errors.
- All 13 experimental datasets are Java, limiting language generalization.
- Some candidate implementations cannot run on every dataset because of source-code and
  dataset-structure constraints.
- The conclusions depend on the selected datasets, metrics, parameters and candidate methods.

## Additional limitations identified for DDTA comparison

- The literature window ends in 2023 and the experiments do not evaluate the subsequent
  generation of RAG and LLM-assisted methods.
- The study focuses only on requirement-to-code links.
- It does not evaluate documentation-as-code, graph provenance, security findings or threat models.
- It reports predictive performance but not analyst review effort, explanation quality,
  calibration, cross-project transfer or stale-link maintenance.
- The companion repository is not a self-contained reproduction environment at the pinned reference.
- A ranking described as SOTA in 2024 must not be presented as the SOTA in 2026.

## Temporal validity

- Contribution type: systematic selection plus empirical benchmark replication.
- Time sensitivity: high.
- Temporal status: current as a 2024 benchmark; not current as a complete 2026 SOTA ranking.
- Evidence window: literature from 2018 to 2023 and experiments published in July 2024.
- Elements still applicable: benchmark discipline, reproducibility scoring, dataset variation,
  class imbalance, threshold sensitivity, metric selection and explicit baseline versioning.
- Potentially obsolete elements: method rankings, tool versions, pre-LLM feature engineering and
  the treatment of GPT only as future work.
- Contemporary corroboration required: post-2024 transformer, RAG and LLM studies with
  cross-project evaluation.
- Permitted thesis use: establish the pre-LLM and classical-ML benchmark floor and motivate a
  stricter DDTA evaluation protocol; do not cite it as evidence of current LLM performance.

## Relationship with Documentation-Driven Threat Analysis

The paper does not evaluate DDTA. Its value is methodological: it demonstrates why a new method
must be compared under common datasets, ground truth, metrics, thresholds and reproducible
baselines rather than only against convenient historical methods.

### Directly supported propositions

- RC-TLR results vary substantially by dataset.
- Baseline selection can materially affect a claimed improvement.
- Precision, recall and F1 remain common core metrics.
- Class imbalance and threshold selection change method performance.
- Classical IR and ML methods remain credible comparison baselines in the 2024 study.
- Public datasets and real ground truth are explicit benchmark-selection requirements.

### Tensions or contradictions

- The article presents a public resource package, but the pinned repository is not self-contained.
- A per-dataset SOTA index is useful for comparison but risks encouraging dataset-specific tuning.
- High within-dataset results do not establish cross-project generalization.
- Strong relative ranking can coexist with modest absolute performance on larger datasets.

### Research gap left open

A current evaluation is needed for transformer, RAG and LLM-assisted recovery using pinned
implementations, unseen projects, heterogeneous languages and artifacts, calibrated confidence,
review effort and change-aware maintenance.

### Overlay-method implications

- Define separate evaluation tasks for document extraction, trace recovery, finding generation
  and security-requirement derivation.
- Pin every baseline implementation and dataset version.
- Use multiple projects and report per-project results rather than only pooled averages.
- Preserve simple IR and classical-ML baselines alongside newer models.
- Separate threshold tuning data from final evaluation data.
- Measure precision, recall and F1 together with reviewer effort and accepted-candidate yield.
- Include cross-project and unseen-project evaluation.
- Record class balance, negative-pair construction and ground-truth provenance.
- Treat model output as ranked candidates with confidence and review state, not as accepted links.
- Report unavailable dependencies and partial reproduction packages as evidence limitations.

## Candidate thesis claims

The source can support claims about benchmark fragmentation, dataset dependence, classical
baseline performance and experimental design through July 2024. It cannot support claims that
LLMs are superior, that recovered links are valid security evidence, or that DDTA improves
threat-model completeness.
