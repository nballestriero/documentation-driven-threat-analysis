## Four reading questions

### 1. Research problem and contribution

The paper argues that requirements engineering normally models relations between individual
requirements while relations between document views are rarely made explicit. This is a
problem for reuse, conformance analysis and review when several documents, standards,
contractors and abstraction levels interact. The contribution is a document-aware traceability
framework based on layered traceability, a T-Reqs conceptual model formalized in Object-Role
Modelling (ORM), and proof-of-concept graph queries for checking document dependencies.

### 2. Starting artifacts and system representation

The work combines expert interviews, requirements-engineering literature and guidance, and
specification documents from European space projects. The EagleEye reference mission and
applicable ECSS standards provide the case-study material. Documents are modeled as views on
sets of requirements, while individual requirements, requirement sets and documents remain
separate traceable entities. Semi-automated PDF extraction populates a Neo4J graph, but the
paper does not derive a complete system or security-analysis model.

### 3. Traceability, change, automation and human role

Typed relations exist on the document, requirement-set and individual-requirement layers.
Relations on one layer can imply or constrain relations on another layer. The model defines
constraints and derivation rules that can be translated into graph queries for consistency and
conformance checks. PDF structure and containment can be extracted semi-automatically, while
many semantic links require manual creation and domain expertise. Baselines and document
versions can be treated as distinct traceable entities; differentiating editorial from semantic
change remains future work.

### 4. Findings, limitations and relationship with DDTA / overlay

The EagleEye proof of concept shows that graph queries can expose missing or conflicting
standard-tailoring information. One query reports seven of eleven applicable-standard links
without an explicit tailoring. The study does not establish precision, recall, usability or
industrial effectiveness because the dataset is small, the ground truth is incomplete, the
domain is narrow and the framework is mostly theoretical. For DDTA, the source supports
first-class document views, typed cross-layer relations and explicit consistency rules, but it
does not evaluate threat analysis or methodology overlays.

## Research method and corpus

The authors derive the conceptual model through literature and guideline analysis, analysis of
specification documents from several space-engineering projects, and expert interviews and
discussions. They instantiate a subset of the model with EagleEye and ECSS material in Neo4J.
The source PDFs are processed with a custom importer; graph queries implement selected
constraints and derivations. The case study is explicitly presented as a proof of concept.

## Contribution

- A combined model of requirements, requirement sets and document views.
- Explicit layered traceability across representation granularities.
- Typed relations whose dependencies can be expressed as constraints and derivation rules.
- A formalized standard-tailoring process for reuse and conformance analysis.
- A graph-based proof of concept with reproducible schema and Cypher queries.

## Findings

- Document content, structure and relations carry semantics beyond the underlying requirement model.
- Documents and sections can be traceable objects rather than passive containers.
- Relations at document, set and requirement granularity influence one another.
- Typed semantics are necessary for meaningful consistency and conformance checks.
- Heterogeneous legacy PDFs limit automatic extraction of semantic trace information.
- Graph queries can identify missing tailoring and other dependency inconsistencies.

## Limitations stated by the authors

The EagleEye dataset is an artificial but industry-informed reference case. The study covers one
domain, uses a small dataset, lacks a complete ground truth and cannot measure precision,
recall or usability. The interview group is small. The framework remains mostly theoretical and
has not been integrated into a practical requirements-engineering tool or workflow. Evaluation
times were not measured, even though the case-study queries showed no observable delay.

## Relationship with Documentation-Driven Threat Analysis

The paper is directly relevant to the DDTA distinction between governed documentation,
canonical model entities and derived views. It supports treating a document and its governed
structure as a traceable representation rather than flattening it into isolated requirements.
It also supports graph constraints that check whether coarse and fine-grained relations agree.
It does not establish that documentation is sufficient to derive Base Analysis, nor that a
security overlay improves threat-model quality.

### Directly supported propositions

- Documents can be modeled as views on requirement sets and as traceable objects in their own right.
- Traceability relations can exist at multiple representation granularities.
- Relations and constraints can propagate or interact across these layers.
- Graph queries can operationalize selected conformance and completeness checks.
- Legacy document heterogeneity constrains automatic extraction.

### Tensions or contradictions

- A rich traceability model can itself become difficult to use and maintain.
- The conceptual schema is more expressive than the selected graph implementation.
- The proof of concept depends on manual semantic-link creation and domain expertise.
- Generalization beyond standardized space engineering is argued but not empirically demonstrated.

### Research gap left open

A practical, evaluated method is still needed for extracting and maintaining document-aware,
typed and versioned relations in modern documentation-as-code environments, while preserving
view semantics and controlling model complexity.

### Overlay-method implications

- Declare the representation layer of every overlay input and output.
- Treat documents, sections, model entities and findings as different traceable objects.
- Define relation types by semantics, not by a generic `link` label.
- Express cross-layer invariants and derivations as testable rules.
- Keep source views and canonical model entities distinct but connected.
- Record baselines and versions as part of trace identity.
- Separate automatically extracted structure from reviewed semantic relations.
- Evaluate usability, correctness and maintenance cost on realistic multi-project corpora.

## Candidate thesis claims

The source can support claims that document views require explicit traceability semantics and
that cross-layer graph constraints can expose selected inconsistencies. It cannot support claims
that the method is effective at industrial scale, that its queries outperform manual review, or
that the approach is valid for DDTA threat overlays without further evaluation.
