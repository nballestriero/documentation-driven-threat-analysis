## Four reading questions

### 1. Research problem and contribution

Publisher material describes 30 reusable requirement patterns intended to improve specification quality. Each pattern is presented as guidance on what a requirement should convey, questions to ask, pitfalls, related/additional requirements and adaptation advice.

### 2. Starting artifacts and system representation

The approach starts from a requirement-authoring situation rather than from raw project semantics or a problem-domain classifier. The publisher sample also covers the structure and supporting contents of a requirements specification. Full chapters on pattern concepts/use were not available in this review.

### 3. Traceability, change, automation and human role

The accessible material describes human authoring guidance. It does not establish automated derivation, provenance or project-authority semantics.

### 4. Findings, limitations and relationship with DDTA / overlay

For DDTA the source is downstream of semantic recognition: once project meaning is governed, authoring patterns may help completeness/readability. It does not show that selecting a requirement pattern proves the underlying project meaning or should produce BA content.

## Research method and corpus

Practitioner pattern catalogue; publisher sample/description reviewed.

## Contribution

Reusable authoring/checking guidance for common requirement situations.

## Findings

- Useful requirement patterns include questions, pitfalls and related requirements rather than sentence templates alone.
- Requirement-pattern use is an authoring activity, distinct from discovering the underlying semantic problem class.
