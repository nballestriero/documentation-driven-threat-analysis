## Four reading questions

### 1. Research problem and contribution

The article updates the state of the art of pre-RS traceability through a systematic
review of 77 publications from 1992 to June 2022. It synthesizes use cases, benefits,
problems, solutions, techniques and evaluation gaps.

### 2. Starting artifacts and system representation

Source artifacts include interviews, meeting protocols, decisions, notes, figures,
multimedia and legacy-system documentation. The target is a requirements specification.
The paper studies trace-link strategies rather than deriving a system model.

### 3. Traceability, change, automation and human role

The review covers definition, production and recovery of links, online/offline capture,
full/selective tracing, impact analysis, history and evolution. Inadequate maintenance,
obsolete links and insufficient version support remain open. Automation can reduce effort,
but inaccurate candidate links reduce trust and preserve the need for human review.

### 4. Findings, limitations and relationship with DDTA / overlay

People-related barriers, perceived workload, trust, adaptability and versioning remain
important. Numerous techniques exist, but industrial evaluations are limited. DDTA should
therefore use typed and versioned links, capture them as a by-product where possible, and
separate automatic candidates from reviewed evidence. The review does not evaluate threat
modeling or methodology overlays directly.

## Research method and corpus

The authors define an SLR protocol, use keyword search and snowballing, apply inclusion
criteria, and perform qualitative data analysis on 77 papers. The corpus ends in June 2022.

## Contribution

- Systematic summary of use cases, benefits, problems and solutions.
- Identification of project, user and usage-goal factors.
- Classification of techniques and their evaluation state.
- Research agenda for standardization, versioning, ROI, AI and industrial evaluation.

## Findings

- Understanding a requirement's source, identifying responsibility and supporting impact
  analysis are prominent use cases.
- Human and organizational barriers are more prominent than purely technical barriers.
- Versioning, adaptability and trust in automatically produced links remain unresolved.
- Many techniques are proposed, but only a small subset has extensive or multi-case evaluation.
- A gap persists between scientific solutions and industrial implementation.

## Limitations stated by the authors

The corpus covers 1992 to June 2022, focuses mainly on software, and does not support
claims about hardware projects or traceability during user-story creation and maintenance.

## Relationship with Documentation-Driven Threat Analysis

The paper strongly supports the need for explicit source/target artifact types, link types,
capture rules, versioning and human review. It does not prove that documentation can be
converted into a security-analysis model, nor that an overlay improves threat analysis.

### Directly supported propositions

- Pre-RS traceability handles heterogeneous and often unstructured source artifacts.
- Impact analysis, responsibility and history are recurring use cases.
- Human effort and trust are central adoption barriers.
- Extensive industrial evaluation is scarce.

### Tensions or contradictions

- The evidence window predates the most recent LLM and RAG advances.
- User-story traceability is explicitly outside the study scope.

### Research gap left open

A current, validated method is still needed for capturing, versioning and evaluating
trace links in modern Git, DevOps and documentation-as-code environments.

### Overlay-method implications

- Declare source and target artifacts and relation semantics.
- Capture links online as a by-product where possible.
- Version links and expose stale/obsolete status.
- Treat automation output as candidate evidence requiring review.
- Evaluate with realistic projects, effort metrics and industrially relevant baselines.

## Candidate thesis claims

The review can support claims about the state of pre-RS traceability through June 2022.
Claims about 2023-2026 AI-assisted traceability require additional recent studies.
