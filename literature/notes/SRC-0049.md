## Four reading questions

### 1. Research problem and contribution

The paper asks whether reusable generic models of application classes can reduce the cost of acquiring domain knowledge during requirements engineering and support requirements capture, validation and explanation. Its contribution is a domain theory with a modelling meta-schema, a hierarchy/library of generic Object System Models (OSMs), a computational analogical matcher, and critique/explanation support. A central contribution for R23 is that it does not rely on names alone: the model hierarchy is discriminated by structural and behavioural knowledge types and by critical-difference rules.

### 2. Starting artifacts and system representation

The starting material is a small and progressively elaborated set of facts about a new application. The tool classifies those facts into knowledge types such as objects, structure objects, state transitions, activities, relationships, goals, events/conditions and properties. The matcher compares that partial input model against generic models and computes a goodness of fit. High-level structural facts and state transitions are strongest discriminators; lower-level goals, events, properties and labels refine the match. The source therefore assumes analyst-mediated fact capture rather than deriving a model directly from heterogeneous project documents.

### 3. Traceability, change, automation and human role

AIR and the requirements critic automate candidate retrieval, explanation of mappings, detection of unmatched/inconsistent facts and guided fact capture. Human judgment remains explicit: the requirements engineer confirms or rejects candidate matches, enters additional facts and iterates until satisfied. The reported workflow is mixed-initiative rather than an automatic 'pattern match -> requirement' transformation. The paper does not provide DDTA-style assertion provenance, authority states or lifecycle change invalidation.

### 4. Findings, limitations and relationship with DDTA / overlay

The paper demonstrates a working matcher/critiquer on a library/stock-control scenario and reports empirical categorisation work, including expert card-sorting that supported part of the proposed library. It also states material limits: existence of a finite generic-model set remains open; stable examples are not proof of completeness; subclasses were not equivalently tested; a complete library would require substantial resources; industrial-strength usability and integration were future work. For DDTA, the strongest evidence is for discriminators, candidate matching, explanation and human confirmation—not for treating a catalogue as exhaustive or authoritative.

## Research method and corpus

Theory construction from knowledge acquisition and prior cases, computational prototype, case-study dialogue, and reported empirical categorisation/card-sorting studies.

## Contribution

A reusable problem/domain classification theory with explicit matching semantics, critical differences, explanation and mixed-initiative review.

## Findings

- Deep structural similarity can support cross-domain reuse even when surface vocabulary differs.
- Candidate classes can be discriminated by ordered semantic features rather than by names alone.
- Matcher output is reviewable and revisable; unmatched facts can remain visible instead of being forced into a class.
- The library's coverage and completeness are unresolved research questions.

## Limitations stated by the authors / accessible source

- No evidence that the catalogue is complete or finite.
- Subclasses were less thoroughly tested than top-level abstractions.
- Prototype/tool evidence is historical and not evidence of current industrial usability.
- No documentation-to-fact extraction, project-authority governance, or change/staleness model.

## Relationship with Documentation-Driven Threat Analysis

### Directly supported propositions

- Reusable semantic classes can be matched from structured facts using discriminating structural/behavioural features.
- A useful matcher can surface both fit and inconsistency and keep a human in the decision loop.
- A pattern/model library need not be treated as exhaustive.

### Tensions or contradictions

- The source presumes facts already expressed in its modelling ontology; DDTA begins earlier from governed project documentation.
- Its generic models are domain-knowledge assets, whereas DDTA must keep external pattern knowledge non-authoritative.

### Research gap left open

How heterogeneous, versioned project documentation yields trustworthy facts with provenance, ambiguity and authority states before matching.

### Overlay-method implications

A DDTA aid could use pattern-like models to ask questions or rank candidate semantic classes, but acceptance must remain explicit and must not emit BA propositions automatically.

## Candidate thesis claims

- Semantic discriminators and critical differences are more defensible than label matching for recurring application classes.
- Human confirmation is part of the reported reuse workflow.
- The paper does not establish a complete or finite catalogue.
