## Four reading questions

### 1. Research problem and contribution

The paper investigates why requirements traceability was difficult in practice and
introduces the influential distinction between pre-RS and post-RS traceability.
Its contribution is both conceptual and empirical, but the empirical context is historical.

### 2. Starting artifacts and system representation

The paper considers requirements, their sources, contributors, refinement history and
downstream development artifacts. It does not define a system model or DFD; it studies
the provenance network around the requirements specification.

### 3. Traceability, change, automation and human role

It separates the life of a requirement before inclusion in the specification from its
later realization and maintenance. Origin, contribution, responsibility and change
propagation are important. The tool and process observations belong to the early 1990s.

### 4. Findings, limitations and relationship with DDTA / overlay

The paper is a foundation for provenance, not current evidence about Git, DevOps,
documentation-as-code, NLP or LLMs. DDTA may use its concepts only together with
contemporary corroboration such as SRC-0021.

## Temporal validity

- Contribution type: foundational conceptual and historical empirical research.
- Time sensitivity: high for tools and practices; lower for the conceptual distinction.
- Temporal status: partially current.
- Evidence window: early 1990s.
- Elements still applicable: pre-RS/post-RS distinction; origin, context and responsibility;
  need to maintain links during change.
- Potentially obsolete elements: tool landscape, operational cost assumptions and automation.
- Contemporary corroboration: SRC-0021 and, for practice, SRC-0022.
- Permitted thesis use: definition and historical context, not standalone evidence of current practice.

## Relationship with Documentation-Driven Threat Analysis

The source supports a navigable provenance chain before and after a requirement. It does
not define document-derived Base Analysis, staleness semantics or a threat-method overlay.

### Directly supported propositions

- Provenance before the specification is distinct from downstream traceability.
- Origin and people involved are important for understanding a requirement.
- Traceability must be maintained during evolution.

### Tensions or contradictions

- The empirical context is technologically outdated.
- The paper does not cover modern repository and AI-assisted workflows.

### Research gap left open

How to capture and maintain provenance as a low-cost by-product in modern, versioned,
documentation-driven development remains open.

### Overlay-method implications

DDTA should record source identity, responsibility, version and verification state for
links used by an overlay. Formal validity alone must not imply evidential completeness.

## Candidate thesis claims

Use only for the origin and definition of pre-RS traceability. Any claim about current
practice requires a modern source.
