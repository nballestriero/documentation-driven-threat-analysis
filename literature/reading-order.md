# Reading order

## Tier 1 — Establish the conceptual baseline
1. Shostack, *Threat Modeling: Designing for Security*.
2. OWASP Threat Modeling Project and Cheat Sheet.
3. van Lamsweerde, *Elaborating Security Requirements by Construction of Intentional Anti-Models*.
4. Sindre and Opdahl, *Eliciting Security Requirements with Misuse Cases*.
5. Gotel and Finkelstein, *An Analysis of the Requirements Traceability Problem*.

## Tier 2 — Position DDTA against model-driven approaches
6. Basin, Doser, and Lodderstedt, *Model Driven Security*.
7. Jürjens, *UMLsec*.
8. Lodderstedt, Basin, and Doser, *SecureUML*.
9. LINDDUN methodology sources.
10. Threat Modeling Manifesto.

## Tier 3 — Continuous and tool-supported practice
11. OWASP DevSecOps threat-modeling guidance.
12. OWASP Threat Dragon.
13. pyTM and SecOpsTM documentation.
14. NIST Secure Software Development Framework.

## Tier 4 — AI-assisted analysis and AI-system security
15. Systematic surveys of LLMs for cybersecurity.
16. OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications.
17. MITRE ATLAS.
18. Recent empirical evaluations of LLM-assisted threat modeling.

## Reading objective

For every source, answer:

- What artifact does the method start from?
- Is the system model assumed, manually authored, extracted, or derived?
- How are threats linked to requirements and evidence?
- How is change handled?
- Which claims are automated, reviewed, or accepted by experts?
- What would distinguish DDTA from this work?
