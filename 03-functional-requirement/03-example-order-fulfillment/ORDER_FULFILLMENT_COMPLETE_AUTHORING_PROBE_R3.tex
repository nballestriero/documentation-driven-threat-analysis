\section{Independent Validation Corpus - Order Fulfillment and Warehouse
Management}\label{independent-validation-corpus---order-fulfillment-and-warehouse-management}

\textbf{COMPLETE MR / DECISION / FR AUTHORING PROBE - REVISION 3}

\begin{quote}
This corpus replaces the two earlier simplified order/inventory holdouts
for validation purposes. It is written against the regular
\texttt{MR\ -\textgreater{}\ Decision\ -\textgreater{}\ FR} hierarchy.
It is evidence/test material, not a universal domain reference
architecture.
\end{quote}

\subsection{1. Problem framing}\label{1-problem-framing}

\textbf{Problem / class of problems:} enable customers to place orders
for physical products and progress them to fulfillment while preserving
coherent commitments across finite stock, concurrent demand, payment
authorization and physical dispatch.

\textbf{Meaningful boundary:} from checkout/order evaluation through
order commitment, warehouse stock movement/reservation, payment
authorization/settlement and handoff to dispatch.

\textbf{Out of scope:} procurement planning, supplier replenishment,
long-term demand forecasting and carrier transport after governed
handoff.

\textbf{Not assumed at framing level:} local vs external WMS/ERP,
payment provider, carrier, database, API protocol, message broker,
microservices, cloud, reservation algorithm, warehouse topology or
persistence technology.

\subsection{2. Operating scenario}\label{2-operating-scenario}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  products are physical and stock is finite;
\item
  concurrent checkout sessions may request the same SKU;
\item
  stock changes through receipts, issues, returns, damage/quarantine and
  controlled adjustments;
\item
  orders may contain multiple lines;
\item
  payment authorization may be authorized, declined or indeterminate;
\item
  retries must not create duplicate financial operations;
\item
  stock reservations are temporary until commit, release or expiry;
\item
  fulfillment may encounter short picks/damage despite a prior
  reservation;
\item
  carrier interaction can be accepted, rejected or indeterminate.
\end{itemize}

\section{5. MR-1 - Order lifecycle and
commitment}\label{5-mr-1---order-lifecycle-and-commitment}

\subsection{Intent}\label{intent}

Manage a coherent order lifecycle and determine when the project is
allowed to make a customer commitment, using inventory and payment
capabilities without absorbing their internal responsibilities.

\subsection{Context}\label{context}

Checkout can fail because stock cannot be reserved, payment cannot be
authorized or a dependent capability returns an indeterminate result.
Commitment must not leave hidden stock/payment side effects.

\subsection{Stakeholders}\label{stakeholders}

Customers, customer support, commercial/order operations, downstream
inventory/payment/fulfillment owners.

\subsection{Scope}\label{scope}

Order evaluation/session, commitment decision,
accepted/rejected/cancelled state and coordination/compensation of
dependent capabilities. Warehouse accounting, PSP internals and carrier
execution remain in their own branches.

\subsection{ADR-1.1 - Explicit correlated OrderEvaluation before
commitment}\label{adr-11---explicit-correlated-orderevaluation-before-commitment}

\textbf{Context.} The project could mutate a draft Order directly, keep
checkout state externally/statelessly, or create an explicit evaluation
context used to correlate stock/payment outcomes before commitment.

\textbf{Decision.} Use one explicit \texttt{OrderEvaluation} identity
for each checkout evaluation. All reservation, payment and compensation
results used for that attempt are correlated to it before an Order
becomes committed.

\textbf{Consequences.} Correlation and idempotency become explicit
project semantics; stale/superseded evaluations can be distinguished
from the current attempt.

\subsubsection{FR-1.1 - Create and preserve one correlated order
evaluation}\label{fr-11---create-and-preserve-one-correlated-order-evaluation}

When Customer submits checkout with one or more product lines and
quantities, OrderService MUST create exactly one active
\texttt{OrderEvaluation} with stable correlation identity and MUST
preserve the evaluated product/quantity set until the evaluation is
terminal or superseded.

\subsection{ADR-1.2 - Reservation and payment authorization are both
commitment
gates}\label{adr-12---reservation-and-payment-authorization-are-both-commitment-gates}

\textbf{Context.} The project could confirm before payment, confirm
before stock allocation, or require both results. Execution may be
sequential or concurrent, but customer commitment must not exist without
both prerequisites.

\textbf{Decision.} An Order may become accepted only after one valid
full-order \texttt{ReservationResult=reserved} and one
\texttt{PaymentAuthorizationResult=authorized} are correlated to the
same active OrderEvaluation. The acquisition order/concurrency of the
two results is not fixed here.

\textbf{Consequences.} MR-1 consumes capabilities from MR-2 and MR-3.
Indeterminate results keep the evaluation non-committed. Side effects
require compensation if the other gate fails.

\subsubsection{FR-1.2 - Obtain governed inventory and payment results
for the active
evaluation}\label{fr-12---obtain-governed-inventory-and-payment-results-for-the-active-evaluation}

For an active OrderEvaluation, OrderService MUST request the required
stock-reservation and payment-authorization services and MUST associate
each returned governed result with the same evaluation before commitment
is decided. A result correlated to another/superseded evaluation MUST
NOT satisfy the current gate.

\subsection{ADR-1.3 - All-or-nothing customer commitment for requested
lines}\label{adr-13---all-or-nothing-customer-commitment-for-requested-lines}

\textbf{Context.} A multi-line order could accept available lines and
backorder/reject others, or require the whole requested set before
commitment.

\textbf{Decision.} The probe uses all-or-nothing customer commitment:
every requested line/quantity must be covered by the reservation used
for acceptance.

\textbf{Consequences.} Partial warehouse allocations cannot silently
become a partially accepted customer order under this project policy.

\subsubsection{FR-1.3 - Commit or reject the full evaluated order from
correlated
results}\label{fr-13---commit-or-reject-the-full-evaluated-order-from-correlated-results}

When both governed gate results are available for the same active
OrderEvaluation, OrderService MUST transition/create
\texttt{Order=ACCEPTED} only if payment is authorized and reservation
covers the full evaluated line set. A negative result MUST prevent
acceptance. An indeterminate result MUST keep the order non-committed
until resolved or the evaluation is terminated by applicable recovery
policy.

\subsection{ADR-1.4 - Explicit compensation of pre-commit side
effects}\label{adr-14---explicit-compensation-of-pre-commit-side-effects}

\textbf{Context.} Reservation or payment authorization can succeed while
the other gate later fails/cancels. Ignoring the successful side effect
would leak stock holds or financial authorizations.

\textbf{Decision.} A non-accepted evaluation with created pre-commit
side effects must explicitly invoke the corresponding release/void
service and track unresolved compensation.

\textbf{Consequences.} Order failure is not treated as a single local
calculation; compensation is a governed cross-MR interaction.

\subsubsection{FR-1.4 - Compensate failed/cancelled
commitment}\label{fr-14---compensate-failedcancelled-commitment}

When an OrderEvaluation terminates without acceptance after a stock
Reservation or Payment Authorization side effect exists, OrderService
MUST request the corresponding release/void capability and MUST retain
enough correlation to determine the compensation outcome. It MUST NOT
treat the evaluation as cleanly compensated while a required
compensation result remains indeterminate.

\section{6. MR-2 - Warehouse and inventory
management}\label{6-mr-2---warehouse-and-inventory-management}

\subsection{Intent}\label{intent-1}

Maintain trustworthy operational stock and provide
availability/reservation/issue behavior that prevents the project from
promising the same finite quantity to incompatible commitments.

\subsection{Context}\label{context-1}

Stock enters/leaves the warehouse, may be damaged/blocked, may be
temporarily reserved by concurrent sessions and must eventually be
consumed or released. Availability is not equivalent to the last counted
on-hand quantity.

\subsection{Stakeholders}\label{stakeholders-1}

Warehouse operators, inventory managers, order operations, fulfillment
operations, finance/audit roles affected by stock discrepancies.

\subsection{Scope}\label{scope-1}

Accountable stock movements, stock-state semantics, availability,
reservation lifecycle, concurrency and issue. Procurement/supplier
planning is outside this probe.

\subsection{ADR-2.1 - Project-owned internal inventory
authority}\label{adr-21---project-owned-internal-inventory-authority}

\textbf{Context.} The project could manage stock internally, delegate
stock/reservation to an external WMS/ERP, or consume a black-box
reservation service. These alternatives move the responsibility boundary
and materially change downstream FRs.

\textbf{Decision.} This concrete probe implements inventory management
internally: \texttt{InventoryService\ +\ InventoryLedger} are the
authoritative project-managed source for on-hand/reserved/blocked stock
state used by ordering and fulfillment.

\textbf{Consequences.} The project owns
receipt/adjustment/availability/reservation/concurrency/release/issue
FRs. A future external-WMS Decision would replace many of these with
integration/API/contract FRs rather than being a transparent
implementation swap.

\subsubsection{FR-2.1 - Record inbound stock receipt exactly
once}\label{fr-21---record-inbound-stock-receipt-exactly-once}

When WarehouseOperator confirms receipt of a product quantity at a
governed stock location, InventoryService MUST create an attributable
inbound \texttt{StockMovement} and MUST increase corresponding on-hand
\texttt{InventoryBalance} exactly once. Reprocessing the same governed
receipt identity MUST NOT increase stock a second time.

\subsection{ADR-2.2 - Accountable stock changes are recorded as
movements, not destructive balance
rewrites}\label{adr-22---accountable-stock-changes-are-recorded-as-movements-not-destructive-balance-rewrites}

\textbf{Context.} Inventory corrections could overwrite current balance
with little history, or preserve attributable movements from which
current accountable state is updated/reconciled.

\textbf{Decision.} Receipt, issue, return and controlled adjustment are
recorded as attributable \texttt{StockMovement} events; historical
accountable movements are not silently rewritten/deleted to hide
previous state.

\textbf{Consequences.} Reconciliation/auditability improve; corrections
are additional governed movements. Future GovernanceRequirements may add
approval/evidence constraints.

\subsubsection{FR-2.2 - Record controlled stock
adjustment}\label{fr-22---record-controlled-stock-adjustment}

When a physical count, damage/quarantine event or approved correction
requires an accountable change, InventoryService MUST create an
adjustment StockMovement linked to the applicable reason/approval
context and MUST update the affected InventoryBalance without deleting
or rewriting the prior accountable movement history.

\subsection{ADR-2.3 - Separate on-hand, reserved and blocked stock
semantics}\label{adr-23---separate-on-hand-reserved-and-blocked-stock-semantics}

\textbf{Context.} One quantity cannot distinguish physically present
stock from stock already promised to orders or present but unusable due
to damage/quarantine.

\textbf{Decision.} Govern distinct on-hand, active-reserved and blocked
quantities. Allocatable availability excludes active reservations and
blocked quantity.

\textbf{Consequences.} Every movement/reservation/release/issue must
preserve these semantics; exact persistence schema remains
implementation.

\subsubsection{FR-2.3 - Produce attributable current availability
without freezing
stock}\label{fr-23---produce-attributable-current-availability-without-freezing-stock}

When an availability query identifies product, location scope and
requested quantity, InventoryService MUST derive
\texttt{AvailabilityResult} from current accountable InventoryBalance,
distinguish sufficient/insufficient/indeterminate and make the result
attributable to its observation/state context. The availability query
MUST NOT itself freeze quantity.

\subsection{ADR-2.4 - Temporary expiring reservation hold bound to one
OrderEvaluation}\label{adr-24---temporary-expiring-reservation-hold-bound-to-one-orderevaluation}

\textbf{Context.} The project could decrement stock immediately at
checkout, use non-expiring allocations, perform best-effort availability
checks without holds, or use temporary reservation/lease semantics.

\textbf{Decision.} Use a temporary \texttt{Reservation} bound to one
OrderEvaluation. ACTIVE reservation freezes allocatable quantity without
representing physical stock issue. It remains until commit/consumption,
explicit release or governed expiry.

\textbf{Consequences.} Reservation identity/lifecycle/correlation become
operational semantics. The expiry duration itself is not hard-coded into
this FR corpus.

\subsubsection{FR-2.4 - Maintain reservation identity and lifecycle
correlation}\label{fr-24---maintain-reservation-identity-and-lifecycle-correlation}

For every ACTIVE Reservation, InventoryService MUST preserve exactly one
originating OrderEvaluation reference, reserved line quantities,
lifecycle state and applicable expiry context. Release, expiry or
consumption commands MUST target that Reservation identity and MUST be
idempotent for repeated delivery of the same governed command.

\subsection{ADR-2.5 - Atomic all-lines reservation under
concurrency}\label{adr-25---atomic-all-lines-reservation-under-concurrency}

\textbf{Context.} Concurrent checkouts can observe the same apparent
availability and oversell if read/check and reservation are independent.
Multi-line orders also risk partial holds that violate MR-1
all-or-nothing commitment.

\textbf{Decision.} Allocation for one full-order ReservationRequest is
atomic with respect to competing reservations and stock-changing
operations: either the complete requested set becomes reserved or no
line becomes ACTIVE for that request.

\textbf{Consequences.} Implementation must provide concurrency control
but the Decision does not prescribe locks, isolation level, CAS, queue
or storage engine.

\subsubsection{FR-2.5 - Create one atomic temporary reservation for the
full evaluated
set}\label{fr-25---create-one-atomic-temporary-reservation-for-the-full-evaluated-set}

When a ReservationRequest identifies an active OrderEvaluation and its
full product/quantity set, InventoryService MUST atomically determine
allocatability against current on-hand/reserved/blocked state. If fully
allocatable, it MUST create exactly one ACTIVE Reservation, increase
reserved quantity for each line exactly once and return
\texttt{ReservationResult=reserved}. If not fully allocatable, it MUST
create no active partial reservation for that request and return
unavailable. A concurrent request MUST NOT reserve quantity already
frozen by the first active Reservation.

\subsection{ADR-2.6 - Reservation release/expiry restores allocatable

\textbf{Context.} A temporary hold can be released explicitly, expire,
or be consumed. Replayed lifecycle events must not double-release
quantity.

\textbf{Decision.} Release and expiry transition an ACTIVE Reservation
out of commitment eligibility exactly once and restore reserved quantity
to allocatable state unless another stock constraint blocks it.

\textbf{Consequences.} Reservation lifecycle commands/results must be
correlated and idempotent. The policy determining expiry time remains
separately governed.

\subsubsection{FR-2.6 - Release reserved quantity after cancellation,
failure or
expiry}\label{fr-26---release-reserved-quantity-after-cancellation-failure-or-expiry}

When an ACTIVE Reservation is explicitly released because its order
evaluation is cancelled/rejected, or when the governed expiry condition
occurs, InventoryService MUST transition it so it can no longer support
commitment and MUST decrease corresponding reserved quantities exactly
once. Replayed release/expiry handling MUST NOT free the same quantity
twice.

\subsection{ADR-2.7 - Physical handoff is the stock-issue
milestone}\label{adr-27---physical-handoff-is-the-stock-issue-milestone}

\textbf{Context.} Accountable stock issue could be recorded at pick
completion, packing, carrier request, carrier acceptance or physical
handoff. Recording too early can reduce on-hand stock when goods remain
controlled by the warehouse.

\textbf{Decision.} For this probe, accountable outbound issue occurs at
the governed \textbf{physical handoff milestone} for the picked
quantities, not merely at dispatch-request preparation or raw carrier
acceptance.

\textbf{Consequences.} Fulfillment must emit/confirm the handoff
milestone. Inventory consumes only quantities covered by the valid
Reservation. Payment capture policy can reference the same governed
milestone.

\subsubsection{FR-2.7 - Convert reserved quantities into accountable

When FulfillmentService confirms the governed physical handoff milestone
for an accepted order under a valid Reservation, InventoryService MUST
create the attributable outbound StockMovement and MUST atomically
reduce on-hand and reserved quantities for the consumed reservation
lines exactly once. It MUST NOT issue quantities beyond the Reservation
or silently consume unrelated unreserved stock.

\subsection{6.1 Counterfactual: if ADR-2.1 chose an external
WMS}\label{61-counterfactual-if-adr-21-chose-an-external-wms}

This is not the chosen branch, but it demonstrates why ADR-2.1 is a real
responsibility-boundary Decision.

With an external authoritative WMS, project-owned FRs would shift toward
integration semantics such as:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  submit governed receipt/adjustment requests through the WMS contract;
\item
  request full-order reservation and correlate the WMS result to
  OrderEvaluation;
\item
  map provider-specific reservation statuses to governed project
  semantics;
\item
  resolve indeterminate/time-out outcomes before semantically new
  retries;
\item
  request release of the exact reservation identity;
\item
  confirm/consume stock issue through the external contract.
\end{itemize}

The WMS\textquotesingle s internal ledger schema, lock/isolation
mechanism and internal concurrency algorithm would normally fall outside
project FR ownership unless exposed as contractual properties.

Therefore "internal vs external inventory authority" is not an
implementation detail: it changes the FR responsibility boundary.

\section{7. MR-3 - Payment authorization and
settlement}\label{7-mr-3---payment-authorization-and-settlement}

\subsection{Intent}\label{intent-2}

Provide governed payment authorization/settlement state that order
commitment can consume without adopting provider-specific status as
project semantics.

\subsection{Context}\label{context-2}

External operations can time out after processing; blind retry can
duplicate financial effects. Authorization, capture and void/refund are
distinct lifecycle operations.

\subsection{Stakeholders}\label{stakeholders-2}

Customers, finance, customer support, fraud/payment operations, order
operations.

\subsection{Scope}\label{scope-2}

Authorization request/result, ambiguous outcome resolution, void/capture
and provider integration. Card-network/provider internals remain outside
project boundary.

\subsection{ADR-3.1 - External PSP behind a governed project
contract}\label{adr-31---external-psp-behind-a-governed-project-contract}

\textbf{Context.} Payment could be processed internally or delegated to
different providers with different APIs and state vocabularies.

\textbf{Decision.} Delegate payment operations to external
\texttt{PaymentProvider} through \texttt{PaymentProviderContract}, with
\texttt{PaymentAdapter} owning project-side integration.

\textbf{Consequences.} API interaction, correlation and failure handling
are project FRs; provider internals are not.

\subsubsection{FR-3.1 - Submit idempotently identifiable authorization
request}\label{fr-31---submit-idempotently-identifiable-authorization-request}

When \texttt{PaymentAuthorizationRequest} is issued for an active
OrderEvaluation, PaymentAdapter MUST submit an idempotently identifiable
provider operation through PaymentProviderContract and preserve
correlation to the evaluation.

\textbf{Context.} A timeout can occur after the provider

financial effects.

\textbf{Decision.} For an indeterminate submitted operation, resolve the
same idempotent operation status before starting a semantically new
authorization unless the contract proves it was not accepted.

\textbf{Consequences.} Recovery queries/idempotency keys become
operational contract; retry policy is not a naive loop.

\subsubsection{FR-3.3 - Resolve indeterminate authorization

When authorization outcome is indeterminate after submission,

initiating a semantically new authorization attempt for that

operation was not accepted.

\section{8. MR-4 - Fulfillment and
dispatch}\label{8-mr-4---fulfillment-and-dispatch}

\subsection{Intent}\label{intent-3}

Transform an accepted order backed by valid stock allocation into
controlled physical fulfillment and dispatch while preserving
correlation between order, reservation, picked quantity, stock issue and
carrier handoff.

\subsection{Context}\label{context-3}

Picking can encounter shortage/damage despite reservation. Carrier
request can fail/timeout. Physical stock must not be issued merely
because a dispatch request exists.

\subsection{Stakeholders}\label{stakeholders-3}

Warehouse/fulfillment operators, customers, carrier operations, customer
support.

\subsection{Scope}\label{scope-3}

Fulfillment task, pick/pack confirmation, fulfillment exceptions,
carrier request/acceptance and physical handoff confirmation. Carrier
transport after handoff is outside scope.

\subsection{ADR-4.1 - Fulfillment task requires accepted order plus
valid
reservation}\label{adr-41---fulfillment-task-requires-accepted-order-plus-valid-reservation}

\textbf{Context.} Warehouse execution could pick directly from order
lines or require the exact stock allocation created during commitment.

\textbf{Decision.} Each FulfillmentTask references one accepted Order
and the valid Reservation covering quantities to pick.

\textbf{Consequences.} Warehouse discrepancies can be detected against
the committed allocation; inventory issue traces to reservation
lifecycle.

\subsubsection{FR-4.1 - Create fulfillment task from accepted order and
reservation}\label{fr-41---create-fulfillment-task-from-accepted-order-and-reservation}

When Order is ACCEPTED and its Reservation is valid for the committed
line set, FulfillmentService MUST create exactly one active
FulfillmentTask referencing both and containing quantities to pick. It
MUST NOT create a normal pick task for rejected/non-committed
evaluation.

\subsection{ADR-4.2 - Short pick/damage is an explicit exception, not
silent
substitution}\label{adr-42---short-pickdamage-is-an-explicit-exception-not-silent-substitution}

\textbf{Context.} Physical execution may find missing/damaged quantity
despite prior logical reservation.

\textbf{Decision.} Short pick/damage/missing allocated quantity produces
an explicit fulfillment exception; normal completion cannot silently
substitute unrelated stock commitment or mark full completion.

\textbf{Consequences.} Recovery/backorder/customer handling can be
governed separately; stock/accounting is not silently corrupted.

\subsubsection{FR-4.2 - Confirm picked quantities and surface
reservation
discrepancy}\label{fr-42---confirm-picked-quantities-and-surface-reservation-discrepancy}

When WarehouseOperator confirms picking for a FulfillmentTask,
FulfillmentService MUST record picked quantities by order line and
compare them with the referenced Reservation. A short
pick/damaged/missing allocated quantity MUST produce an explicit
blocking fulfillment exception and MUST NOT be silently treated as
complete fulfillment.

\section{10. Validation observations}\label{10-validation-observations}

This corpus supports the regular hierarchy without requiring Decisions
below FR:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  responsibility-boundary choices (internal WMS vs external WMS,
  external PSP, external carrier) sit naturally at Decision level;
\item
  strategy/policy choices (reservation semantics, atomic all-line
  allocation, handoff milestone, capture timing) sit naturally at
  Decision level;
\item
  operational obligations become concrete FRs under those commitments;
\item
  cross-MR composition appears as service/capability consumption rather
  than multiple FR owners;
\item
  no FR needs to own a Decision child;
\item
  mutable values such as reservation duration remain outside FR unless
  later classified by a specialized/policy/interface contract.
\end{itemize}

The exact formal object used to represent cross-MR service consumption
remains deliberately open for the Base Analysis phase.
