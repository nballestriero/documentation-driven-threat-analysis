\section{DDTA - Working Metamodel - Functional
Requirement}\label{ddta---working-metamodel---functional-requirement}

\textbf{DOCUMENTATION-LAYER CLOSURE CANDIDATE - REVISION 2}

This revision supersedes the Revision 1 topology experiment. The two
earlier simplified holdouts that suggested direct
\texttt{MR\ -\textgreater{}\ FR} parentage are excluded from closure
evidence. More realistic access-control and warehouse/order experiments
repeatedly exposed hidden responsibility/solution commitments before
operational FRs could be written. For the thesis baseline, DDTA
therefore adopts a regular
\texttt{MR\ -\textgreater{}\ Decision\ -\textgreater{}\ FR} hierarchy,
with a controlled necessity/default Decision rule for the rare
singleton-solution case.

\subsection{1. Definition}\label{1-definition}

A Functional Requirement is a governed, independently assessable
\textbf{operational obligation} that descends from exactly one Decision
and states one coherent behavior, service, transition or observable
result that the project/system must provide under the governed context
established by its MR and Decision branch.

It must be specific enough to guide realization and verification without
prescribing incidental source-code/framework detail.

\subsubsection{Fundamental question}\label{fundamental-question}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Given the parent Decision and an applicable condition/input,
which governed subject/capability must perform which functional action
on or with which governed object/information, and which observable
outcome or failure behavior must follow?}
\end{quote}

\subsection{2. Hierarchy and ownership}\label{2-hierarchy-and-ownership}

\begin{Shaded}
\begin{Highlighting}[]
\NormalTok{MacroRequirement}
\NormalTok{    |}
\NormalTok{    +{-}{-} Decision}
\NormalTok{           |}
\NormalTok{           +{-}{-} FunctionalRequirement}
\NormalTok{                  |}
\NormalTok{                  +{-}{-} SpecializedRequirement *}
\end{Highlighting}
\end{Shaded}

Closed thesis-baseline rules:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \texttt{FunctionalRequirement.parentDecision\ =\ exactly\ 1};
\item
  owning MR is derived from
  \texttt{parentDecision\ -\textgreater{}\ MacroRequirement} and must
  not become a second topology authority;
\item
  an L2 registry may materialize the MR id as an indexed/mirrored value,
  but it is derived;
\item
  no Decision is a child of FR;
\item
  FR may have \texttt{0..*} Specialized Requirements;
\item
  the thesis next formal specialization is \texttt{SecurityRequirement};
  Governance/Privacy/Performance remain future/generalization work
  unless needed by a concrete counterexample.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{3. Semantic shape}\label{3-semantic-shape}

\begin{Shaded}
\begin{Highlighting}[]
\NormalTok{FunctionalRequirement}
\NormalTok{|{-} id / lifecycle                 [common governed{-}document concerns]}
\NormalTok{|{-} title                          [required]}
\NormalTok{|{-} functionalObligation           [required]}
\NormalTok{|{-} functionalClause 1..*}
\NormalTok{|    |{-} normativeStatement        [required]}
\NormalTok{|    \textasciigrave{}{-} spoReference 1..*         [structured governed references]}
\NormalTok{|         |{-} subject}
\NormalTok{|         |{-} predicate}
\NormalTok{|         \textasciigrave{}{-} object}
\NormalTok{|{-} parentDecision {-}\textgreater{} Decision 1}
\NormalTok{\textasciigrave{}{-} specializedRequirement {-}\textgreater{} 0..*}
\end{Highlighting}
\end{Shaded}

No FR-local \texttt{Intent} is required in the core. The MR + Decision
context should explain why the behavior exists; the FR explains what
operationally must happen.

Acceptance/verification evidence and realization artifacts remain
downstream relations, not intrinsic FR fields.

\subsection{4. Operationality rule}\label{4-operationality-rule}

An FR must do more than name a desirable state or capability. It must
expose enough operational semantics that an implementer/reviewer can
understand what must occur.

Strong forms include:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  event/condition -\textgreater{} required transition;
\item
  input/request -\textgreater{} required processing responsibility
  -\textgreater{} observable result;
\item
  lifecycle event -\textgreater{} state change and failure behavior;
\item
  interaction request -\textgreater{} governed output/correlation
  behavior;
\item
  concurrency condition -\textgreater{} externally meaningful
  atomicity/idempotency invariant.
\end{itemize}

Weak forms such as "provide inventory availability" or "keep
authorization updated" are insufficient when they hide responsibility
boundaries, lifecycle, concurrency, correlation or failure semantics
that materially determine implementation and verification.

\subsection{5. Decision coverage test}\label{5-decision-coverage-test}

Before accepting an FR, ask:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  Which parent Decision makes this exact operational responsibility part
  of the project?
\item
  Does the Decision determine an internal vs external responsibility
  boundary, strategy, policy, interface commitment or other material
  narrowing?
\item
  If no genuine alternative can be identified, has the branch documented
  a valid necessity/default Decision rather than silently skipping the
  layer?
\item
  Would changing the parent Decision require replacing or materially
  rewriting this FR? If yes, parentage is semantically strong.
\item
  Is the proposed parent merely a tautology invented to hold the FR? If
  yes, strengthen the Decision commitment or use the necessity/default
  rule transparently.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{6. Normative statement plus SPO
references}\label{6-normative-statement-plus-spo-references}

The \textbf{normative prose is semantically primary}. SPO makes the
participating governed references explicit and reusable; it does not
carry all conditional, concurrency, lifecycle or failure semantics by
itself.

Readable pattern:

\begin{Shaded}
\begin{Highlighting}[]
\NormalTok{[Condition,] \textless{}Subject\textgreater{} MUST \textless{}Predicate\textgreater{} \textless{}Object\textgreater{}}
\NormalTok{[qualifier / observable outcome / failure behavior].}
\end{Highlighting}
\end{Shaded}

Example:

\begin{Shaded}
\begin{Highlighting}[]
\NormalTok{When a ReservationRequest identifies the active order evaluation and full line set,}
\NormalTok{InventoryService MUST create an ACTIVE Reservation only if the complete set can be}
\NormalTok{allocated atomically against current stock state; a concurrent request MUST NOT reserve}
\NormalTok{quantity already frozen by that Reservation.}
\end{Highlighting}
\end{Shaded}

Associated SPO references may include:

\begin{Shaded}
\begin{Highlighting}[]
\NormalTok{InventoryService {-}{-} creates {-}{-}\textgreater{} Reservation}
\NormalTok{InventoryService {-}{-} updates {-}{-}\textgreater{} InventoryBalance}
\NormalTok{InventoryService {-}{-} produces {-}{-}\textgreater{} ReservationResult}
\end{Highlighting}
\end{Shaded}

The structured triples aid identity reuse and tooling, while the
normative statement owns atomicity, correlation and failure semantics.

\subsection{7. FunctionalPredicate
metacontract}\label{7-functionalpredicate-metacontract}

A governed functional predicate has at least:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  stable identity;
\item
  readable label;
\item
  normative meaning;
\item
  functional area;
\item
  compatible subject/object kinds (to be bound formally after the Base
  Analysis taxonomy is closed).
\end{itemize}

The DDTA core does not freeze an exhaustive verb list. Example extension
areas include storage, communication, lifecycle, transformation,
inventory, cryptography, signature and identity/access.

ThreatForge may later offer predicate pickers filtered by subject/object
kinds, but tooling cannot silently invent project semantics.

\subsection{8. Cross-MR service
consumption}\label{8-cross-mr-service-consumption}

MRs may be complementary in an end-to-end service. An FR can consume a
capability owned by another MR branch without gaining a second owner.

For the documentation-layer closure, the \textbf{semantics} are fixed:

\begin{quote}
consuming another branch\textquotesingle s governed capability does not
transfer FR ownership or create a second parent.
\end{quote}

The exact canonical target of the non-hierarchical consumption relation
is deliberately deferred to the Base Analysis phase. It may resolve to a
governed capability/service/BAE/interface relation rather than always to
one provider FR.

This avoids prematurely freezing \texttt{FR\ -\textgreater{}\ FR} as the
only service-composition representation.

\subsection{9. Parameter classification
rule}\label{9-parameter-classification-rule}

No generic \texttt{functionalParameters} field belongs to the FR core.

A changing value is classified by semantics:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  image vs frame-set vs short video -\textgreater{} Decision/strategy;
\item
  response-time target -\textgreater{} quality/performance requirement
  candidate;
\item
  retention duration -\textgreater{} governance/privacy/policy
  candidate;
\item
  API-imposed limit/field -\textgreater{} InterfaceContract candidate;
\item
  buffer size or implementation tuning -\textgreater{}
  realization/configuration.
\end{itemize}

An FR references the applicable governed rule where necessary instead of
copying mutable values by default.

\subsection{10. Closed FR invariants for the documentation
baseline}\label{10-closed-fr-invariants-for-the-documentation-baseline}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Exactly one parent Decision}.
\item
  \textbf{Derived MR ownership} - MR comes from the parent Decision
  branch.
\item
  \textbf{Operational obligation} - FR states
  behavior/service/transition/result, not rationale or architecture
  preference.
\item
  \textbf{Independent assessability} - satisfaction can be meaningfully
  evaluated.
\item
  \textbf{Coherent unit} - one independently meaningful
  capability/behavior per FR.
\item
  \textbf{Decision non-repetition} - FR makes the commitment operational
  rather than restating it.
\item
  \textbf{Implementation independence} - no incidental
  framework/source/class/algorithm detail unless contractual.
\item
  \textbf{Normative prose primary} - SPO supports structured references
  but does not replace full behavior semantics.
\item
  \textbf{SPO consistency} - structured references must not contradict
  normative prose.
\item
  \textbf{Governed-reference reuse} - existing subject/object identities
  are referenced rather than redefined.
\item
  \textbf{No Decision children} - later choices remain MR-level
  Decisions, not descendants of FR.
\item
  \textbf{Specialization downward} - domain-specific constraints
  enrich/constrain FR through Specialized Requirements.
\item
  \textbf{Service-consumption without co-ownership} - consuming another
  MR\textquotesingle s service/capability does not transfer ownership.
\item
  \textbf{Realization separation} - implementation artifacts realize FRs
  but are not FR semantics.
\item
  \textbf{Verification separation} - evidence supports satisfaction but
  is not the FR or its realization.
\item
  \textbf{Method neutrality} - authoring does not require
  STRIDE/STRIDE-AI terminology.
\item
  \textbf{Tool non-authority} - assistance may resolve/suggest/validate
  but cannot silently create governed semantics.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{11. Specialized Requirement
boundary}\label{11-specialized-requirement-boundary}

Conceptually:

\begin{Shaded}
\begin{Highlighting}[]
\NormalTok{FunctionalRequirement}
\NormalTok{      |}
\NormalTok{      +{-}{-} SecurityRequirement *}
\NormalTok{      +{-}{-} GovernanceRequirement *   [future/generalization]}
\NormalTok{      +{-}{-} PrivacyRequirement *      [future/generalization]}
\NormalTok{      \textasciigrave{}{-}{-} Performance/Quality ?     [future/generalization]}
\end{Highlighting}
\end{Shaded}

For thesis scope, only \texttt{SecurityRequirement} is required for the
next closed end-to-end cycle. Its derivation from accepted Findings and
relation to effective obligation will be formalized after Base Analysis
and the overlay/plugin contract.

\subsection{12. Reopen rule}\label{12-reopen-rule}

This documentation-layer baseline is reopenable only by a concrete
counterexample, especially if:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  SecurityRequirement cannot be attached/constrained without corrupting
  FR semantics;
\item
  Base Analysis cannot be derived/integrated from the documented system
  facts without changing MR/Decision/FR authoring;
\item
  a methodology overlay requires information that cannot be expressed
  without violating the current layer boundaries.
\end{itemize}
